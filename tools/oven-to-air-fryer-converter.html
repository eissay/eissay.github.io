How the conversion works

An air fryer is a small convection oven with the heating element inches from the food, so an oven recipe runs hot and fast inside it. Two adjustments fix almost everything: lower the temperature by about 25°F / 15°C, and cut the time by about 20 percent. Then start checking at two-thirds of the converted time — once something over-browns, there is no way back.

When not to convert

Whole birds, large roasts, wet batters and anything cooked in liquid do not convert well: the basket is small, holds one layer, and the element sits close to the food. For the full list of what works and what fails, read the oven to air fryer conversion chart.

Verify doneness, always

The converter gives a starting point, not a finish line. Times vary by wattage and basket size. Use an instant-read thermometer: poultry 165°F / 74°C, ground meats 160°F / 71°C, whole cuts of pork, beef and lamb 145°F / 63°C, fish and shellfish 145°F / 63°C.
