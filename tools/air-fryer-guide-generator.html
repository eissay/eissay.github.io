Trust

Calculated, not generated

There is no AI here and there is no server either: the whole food library and the calculation engine load once and run inside your browser - offline, private, instant, and fully standalone. Every number comes from fixed, reviewable tables:

  • The USDA safe minimum internal temperature table - poultry 165°F, ground meats 160°F, whole cuts 145°F + rest, fish and shellfish 145°F, leftovers 165°F.
  • Tested time tables compiled from manufacturer guidebooks and kitchen practice - the same conservative ranges we publish across the site.
  • Fixed adjustment rules - frozen, marinated, breaded, fryer type, wattage and your goal each shift the numbers by a declared amount you can see.

Questions

Frequently asked

How does the guide generator work?

It is a chat that asks a few quick questions - what you are cooking, its state (frozen, fresh, marinated), the amount, your fryer and your goal. Type your answers, pick from the suggestions as you type, or use the dropdowns. Behind the chat is a fixed calculation engine: the same inputs always produce the same answer.

Can I say everything in one sentence?

Yes - something like “crispy frozen chicken wings, 1 lb, oven style” is parsed in one go and you get the temperatures immediately. The chat only asks about whatever is missing from your sentence.

Does it use AI or send my data anywhere?

No. There is no AI and no server: the entire food library and the calculation engine load once and run inside your browser. It is fully standalone, works offline after the first load, and nothing you type is sent anywhere.

Where do the times and temperatures come from?

Three sources: the USDA safe minimum internal temperature table (poultry 165°F, ground meats 160°F, whole cuts of pork, beef and lamb 145°F with a rest, fish and shellfish 145°F), conservative time ranges compiled from manufacturer guidebooks, and fixed adjustment rules for state, amount, fryer type and goal - each shown on the answer.

Why does meat always say to use a thermometer?

Because air fryers vary in wattage, basket size and airflow far more than most people expect. The times are honest starting points, but the only reliable way to verify doneness is an instant-read thermometer in the thickest piece.

The answer gave me a range, not one number. Which do I use?

Start checking at the low end - early is reversible, burnt is not. The range absorbs real-world variation between machines; your thermometer makes the final call.
