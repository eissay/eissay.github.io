Trust

Calculated, not generated

There is no AI here and there is no server either: the whole food library and the calculation engine load once and run inside your browser — offline, private, instant. Every number comes from fixed, reviewable tables:

  • The USDA safe minimum internal temperature table — poultry 165°F, ground meats 160°F, whole cuts 145°F + rest, fish and shellfish 145°F, leftovers 165°F.
  • Our own 54 tested charts — the same numbers we publish, now adjustable by state and amount.
  • Manufacturer guidebooks for basket and oven-style air fryers, 1000–1800 W.

Every applied adjustment is shown on the guide itself — you always see why a number changed. Times are honest ranges, never fake precision. Data v2026-10-17.

Questions

Frequently asked

How does the guide generator work?

You pick the food, its state (frozen, fresh, marinated), the amount and a few details about your air fryer. The tool looks up tested time and temperature tables, applies fixed adjustment rules for your choices, and assembles a complete guide — the same inputs always produce the same answer.

Does it use AI or send my data anywhere?

No. There is no AI and no server: the entire food library and the calculation engine load once and run inside your browser. It works offline after the first load, and nothing you select is sent anywhere.

Where do the times and temperatures come from?

Three sources: the USDA safe minimum internal temperature table (poultry 165°F, ground meats 160°F, whole cuts of pork, beef and lamb 145°F with a rest, fish and shellfish 145°F), our own library of tested air fryer charts, and manufacturer quick-start guidebooks for basket and oven-style models.

Why does meat always say to use a thermometer?

Because air fryers vary in wattage, basket size and airflow far more than most people expect. The times are honest starting points, but the only reliable way to verify doneness is an instant-read thermometer in the thickest piece.

The generator gave me a range, not one number. Which do I use?

Start checking at the low end — early is reversible, burnt is not. The range absorbs real-world variation between machines; your thermometer makes the final call.
