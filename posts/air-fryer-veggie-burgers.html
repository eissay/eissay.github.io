Air Fryer Veggie Burgers: Frozen and Homemade Times

Air fryer veggie burger times for frozen and homemade patties, with 380°F / 193°C settings, flip handling and the heat-through rule.

Veggie burgers are the patty the air fryer rescues: skillet versions fall apart on the flip, oven versions turn dry, but moving hot air crisps both faces while the center heats through — without touching the patty.

The safety question is different from meat burgers: most frozen veggie patties are fully cooked and only need heating, while homemade ones with egg binders must be cooked through.

Times vary by appliance model. Patty brand, thickness and binder change the clock — follow the package when it gives one, and verify the center is hot.

The heating rule#

Read the package first. The two categories behave differently:

Patty typeThe rule
Frozen, fully cooked (most brands)Heat until hot throughout; brands typically specify heating to 165°F / 74°C
Homemade with egg binderCook through; the egg sets as the patty reaches 165°F / 74°C
Homemade, no egg (binders like flax or oats)Heat until steaming hot in the center

Most frozen veggie patties are pre-cooked at the factory — the air fryer's job is to heat the center through and crisp the surface. The thermometer still decides: a patty heated to 165°F / 74°C in the middle is served safely, whatever the label's suggested minutes.

Times assume a preheated basket and a single layer. Frozen patties go in solid — no thawing; thawed patties slump.

The flip is the delicate moment — veggie patties are softer than beef. A wide spatula and one slow, confident turn at halfway. A metal toast rack under a fragile patty keeps it off the basket wires.

The homemade binder problem#

Homemade veggie patties without enough binder fall apart no matter how carefully they are flipped. The reliable ratio: cooked beans or lentils (dried well), an egg or flax egg, a half cup of breadcrumbs or cooked grain per two cups of vegetables, all pulsed coarsely — not pureed.

Chilling is non-negotiable for homemade patties — a warm patty slumps before it sets. Cook until the center is hot through (165°F / 74°C for egg-bound mixes) and the surface is crisp.

Five mistakes with air fryer veggie burgers#

  1. Thawing frozen patties. They slump; cook straight from the freezer.
  2. Rushing the flip. A wide spatula and one slow turn — veggie patties are softer than beef.
  3. Skipping the chill on homemade patties. Thirty minutes refrigerated firms them.
  4. Overcooking frozen ones dry. They are pre-cooked; heat through, then stop.
  5. Piling toppings on the basket. Cheese for the last 1–2 minutes; cold toppings after.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do veggie burgers take in an air fryer?

10–15 minutes at 380°F / 193°C for frozen standard patties, flipped once. Thick or premium patties need 13–17 minutes; homemade chilled ones 12–16.

Do frozen veggie burgers need to be cooked to a temperature?

Most are fully cooked and only need heating through — but the safe target is the same: 165°F / 74°C hot in the center, which is what most brands specify for heating.

Why did my veggie burger fall apart?

A warm homemade patty or a rushed flip. Chill formed patties 30+ minutes, use a wide spatula, and turn once — slowly.

Can I cook the bun in the basket too?

Yes — split and toast it for the last 2 minutes at 350°F / 177°C, after the patty comes out.

How do I reheat a veggie burger?

380°F / 193°C for 5–7 minutes revives the surface; heat the center through before serving.

Veggie burgers reward gentle handling: frozen patties straight in, one slow flip, heated through at 380°F / 193°C. The air fryer gives them the crisp faces the skillet always ruined.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
