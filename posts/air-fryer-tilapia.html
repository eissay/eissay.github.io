Tilapia is thin, lean and fast — which makes it easy to overcook in an air fryer. Two extra minutes turn a moist fillet into a chalky one. The other half of the story is frozen: most tilapia is sold individually sealed in a steam wrapper, and it cooks perfectly well from frozen with a slightly longer clock.

Both versions share the same settings: 400°F / 205°C, one uncrowded layer, a flip at halfway. What changes is the time, and how you confirm doneness.

This chart covers fresh and frozen fillets, the internal temperature that decides doneness, and the breading habits that keep a crust crisp instead of soggy.

Times vary by appliance model, fillet thickness and basket crowding. Confirm doneness with a thermometer at the thickest point — 145°F / 63°C is the USDA safe minimum for fish.

The 145°F / 63°C fish rule#

Tilapia is done by temperature, not by color: the flesh turns opaque white long before the center is cooked through. An instant-read thermometer in the thickest part of the fillet is the only reliable check.

Pull the fillets at or just above 145°F / 63°C. Below that, they are undercooked; far above, they dry out thread by thread.

Start checking at the low end of every range. A thin fillet near the basket wall runs a minute or two ahead of a thick one in the center, so pull pieces as they hit temperature rather than cooking the whole basket to one clock.

Fresh vs frozen fillets#

Frozen tilapia needs no thawing — the air fryer handles it directly. Two changes matter: the wrapper comes off before anything enters the basket, and the clock runs about 2–4 minutes longer. Treat frozen times as wider ranges and let the thermometer, not the clock, call the end.

If you do thaw, do it in the refrigerator overnight and pat the fillets very dry — thawed fish releases more water than fresh and steams unless you blot it.

Breading that stays crisp#

Breaded tilapia follows the same rule as any wet food in an air fryer: a coating seals in steam unless the surface is dry and the basket has room. Bread in this order — flour, beaten egg, seasoned crumbs pressed on firmly. A light oil spray over the crust browns it. Do not flip early; the crust releases on its own once it sets, usually after 5–6 minutes.

Keep sauces away until serving. Fillets sitting in lemon butter soften within minutes; the crust survives longest on a wire rack or a warm plate.

Five mistakes with air fryer tilapia#

  1. Cooking frozen fillets in their wrapper. The wrapper traps steam around the fish; remove it before the basket.
  2. Guessing doneness by the clock alone. Fillet thickness varies more than any chart can show; confirm 145°F / 63°C at the thickest point.
  3. Overcrowding. Stacked fillets steam each other; one layer with air between them browns evenly.
  4. Skipping the flip. The underside stays pale and soft without one pass at halfway.
  5. Marinating too long in citrus. Lemon and lime firm up the surface in about 30 minutes; marinate 15–30 minutes at most, or season lightly instead.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do you cook tilapia in an air fryer?

8–12 minutes at 400°F / 205°C for fresh fillets and 12–16 minutes for frozen ones, flipping halfway. Confirm 145°F / 63°C at the thickest point.

Can you put frozen tilapia in the air fryer?

Yes — remove the steam wrapper, pat off surface ice, and cook straight from frozen at 400°F / 205°C for 12–16 minutes, about 2–4 minutes longer than fresh.

What temperature is tilapia done?

145°F / 63°C internal, measured at the thickest part — the USDA safe minimum for fish. The flesh should flake easily and look opaque throughout.

How do I reheat tilapia without drying it out?

350°F / 175°C for 4–6 minutes until the center reaches 165°F / 74°C. A microwave warms faster but steams the coating limp.

Why is my breading soggy?

Wet fillets, a crowded basket or no oil spray. Pat the fish dry, flour it first, spread the fillets out and spray the crust lightly before cooking.

Tilapia punishes guesswork and rewards a thermometer: 400°F / 205°C, one uncrowded layer, a flip at halfway, and 145°F / 63°C in the thickest part. Whether the fillet starts fresh or frozen, that combination is what keeps it moist.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
