Chicken tenders are the strip of breast that made air fryers famous: breaded and cooked in moving hot air, they come out crisp on every side with a fraction of the oil of deep frying. Homemade tenders beat frozen on flavor; frozen beats homemade on speed. The air fryer handles both — with the same internal rule.

This chart covers the homemade route, the frozen route and the buttermilk brine that keeps them juicy.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Tender thickness and coating change the clock, but 165°F / 74°C does not change.

The rule for tenders#

Tenders are poultry under a coating, and poultry has one internal temperature under USDA rules:

TenderSafe internal temperature
Homemade breaded tenders165°F / 74°C
Frozen tenders, cooked from frozen165°F / 74°C
Leftover tenders, reheated165°F / 74°C

Probe the thickest tender in the batch — strips vary, and the largest one rules.

Air fryer chicken tenders chart: homemade#

The buttermilk brine is what separates homemade tenders from every fast-food version: thirty minutes in buttermilk (or milk with a spoon of vinegar) tenderizes the strip and helps the coating adhere.

Buttermilk-brined, panko380°F / 193°C10–12 minFlip at halfway
Buttermilk-brined, cracker crumbs380°F / 193°C10–12 minWatch for darkening

The breading sequence: dry the brined tenders, then flour, egg, seasoned breadcrumbs — pressed on firmly. Panko holds its shape in moving air and crisps best; press every flake into the strip.

Air fryer chicken tenders chart: frozen#

Frozen tenders are formulated for the air fryer — straight in, no thawing, no oil.

Frozen tendersTemperatureTimeNotes

Frozen coatings brown before the center finishes — the thermometer matters even more than for homemade. The thickest strip in the basket decides.

Sauce pairing#

Dry rubs and sauces go on after the heat. Buffalo, honey mustard, ranch and honey-garlic all work — tossed hot or served on the side. Saucing during the cook steams the breading soggy, the same rule as every breaded item on this site.

Five mistakes with chicken tenders#

  1. Judging by the crust. Golden breading hides undercooked chicken; probe the thickest one.
  2. Skipping the buttermilk brine. Thirty minutes is what keeps the strip juicy under the crust.
  3. Overcrowding. Tenders touching steam each other; two batches beat one packed basket.
  4. Saucing during the cook. Wet sauce steams the breading; sauce after the heat.
  5. Thawing frozen tenders. They cook from frozen at the same time — thawing just softens them.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do chicken tenders take in an air fryer?

10–12 minutes at 380°F / 193°C for homemade brined tenders, flipped halfway. Frozen ones need 12–17 minutes at 390°F / 199°C. Verify the thickest reaches 165°F / 74°C.

Do I need to brine the tenders?

Thirty minutes in buttermilk is what keeps them juicy — it tenderizes the strip and helps the coating stick. Skip it and the tenders still cook, but they dry faster.

What temperature should chicken tenders be inside?

165°F / 74°C under USDA rules for all poultry — homemade and frozen alike.

Why is my breading soggy?

Overcrowding or saucing during the cook. Spread in one layer with air between, shake at halfway, and sauce after the heat.

Can I make tenders ahead?

Bread them, rest 10 minutes, then freeze on a tray. Cook from frozen at 12–14 minutes at 380°F / 193°C.

Tenders reward the brine and the thermometer: thirty minutes in buttermilk, pressed panko, 380°F / 193°C, probe the thickest one. The air fryer makes both routes better than any deep fryer.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
