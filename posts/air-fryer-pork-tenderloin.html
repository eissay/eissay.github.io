Air Fryer Pork Tenderloin: Time and Temperature Guide

Air fryer pork tenderloin times by weight, with the 145°F / 63°C internal rule, carryover cooking and the rest that keeps it juicy.

Pork tenderloin is a lean, boneless cylinder that fits a basket perfectly and roasts in under twenty minutes. It is also one of the easiest proteins to overcook, because there is almost no fat to forgive a mistake — every minute past done shows up as dryness.

The good news is that the target is lower than most people expect. Under USDA rules, whole cuts of pork are safe at 145°F / 63°C with a three minute rest — juicier than the well-done pork of decades past and just as safe.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Wattage, basket size and the tenderloin's shape change the clock, but 145°F / 63°C plus rest does not change.

The rule for pork tenderloin#

Tenderloin is a whole muscle, not ground meat, so it qualifies for the lower whole-cut standard:

CutSafe internal temperatureRest
Pork tenderloin, whole or medallions145°F / 63°C3 minutes
Pork loin roast, thicker145°F / 63°C3 minutes
Ground pork (not tenderloin)160°F / 71°CNone

Probe the thickest end of the tenderloin — the tail end is always thinner, so it finishes first. Some cooks fold the thin tail under the roast or tuck it beneath to even out the shape; that one move makes the whole piece cook evenly.

Times assume a preheated basket and a whole, untied tenderloin of one to one and a half pounds. Sear-level heat works here because the piece is small and lean.

Start checking four minutes before the low end of the range. Tenderloin climbs quickly — from 145°F / 63°C to 160°F / 71°C can take only four or five more minutes, and those minutes are the difference between juicy and chalky.

The rest is not optional#

The USDA's 145°F / 63°C standard includes a three minute rest, and for good reason: the carryover heat finishes the surface while the juices settle. In practice, a tenderloin pulled at 145°F / 63°C keeps rising during the rest.

Pull from basketTemperature after 5-minute restResult
145°F / 63°CAbout 150°F / 66°CBlush pink center, very juicy
150°F / 66°CAbout 155°F / 68°CBarely pink, firm and juicy
160°F / 71°CAbout 165°F / 74°CWell-done, noticeably drier

All three are safe — the USDA floor is 145°F / 63°C with rest, and anything above it is a texture choice. If you prefer no pink at all, pull at 155°F / 68°C rather than cooking longer in the basket; the rest does the last of the work more gently.

Seasoning that suits a fast roast#

Tenderloin takes a dry rub well: salt, pepper, garlic powder, smoked paprika, a pinch of brown sugar. Rub it and let it sit while the basket preheats — twenty minutes at room temperature is enough. Sugary rubs darken quickly at 400°F / 205°C, so keep the sugar small or drop the temperature to 380°F / 193°C and add three to four minutes.

A quick glaze goes on in the last three minutes: honey, mustard and a splash of vinegar, brushed thin. Anything wetter steams the surface instead of glazing it.

Five mistakes with pork tenderloin#

  1. Cooking to 160°F / 71°C in the basket. That is well-done territory for a whole cut; pull at 145°F / 63°C and rest.
  2. Skipping the rest. The three minute rest is part of the USDA standard, not a suggestion.
  3. Probing the thin tail. The tail finishes ten minutes early; probe the thick end.
  4. Marinating in sugar from the start. Sweet marinades scorch at 400°F / 205°C before the center is done.
  5. Crowding two tenderloins against the walls. Air must reach every side; leave a gap or cook in batches.

Frequently asked questions#

How long does pork tenderloin take in an air fryer?

15–18 minutes at 400°F / 205°C for a one pound tenderloin, 18–22 minutes for one and a half pounds. Check early — the piece cooks fast.

What temperature should pork tenderloin reach?

145°F / 63°C in the thickest part, followed by a three minute rest, per USDA rules for whole cuts of pork. It will stay blush pink inside — that is correct and safe.

Is pink pork safe?

At 145°F / 63°C with a proper rest, yes — USDA guidance for whole cuts says exactly that. Ground pork is the exception: it must reach 160°F / 71°C.

Can I cook frozen pork tenderloin?

Thaw it first. A frozen tenderloin browns long before the center thaws, which makes an accurate reading impossible. Overnight in the refrigerator is enough.

Why is my tenderloin dry even when cooked right?

The piece was larger than the time assumed, or the rest was skipped. Verify with a probe at the thick end and rest five minutes — carryover finishes the job gently.

Lean meat punishes guesswork. Preheat, probe the thick end, pull at 145°F / 63°C, and give the rest its three minutes — the air fryer will return a tenderloin that rivals any oven roast in half the time.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
