Air Fryer Fish: Times and Temperatures for Cod, Tilapia, Haddock and Catfish

Air fryer fish times by thickness for cod, tilapia, haddock and catfish, plus frozen breaded fillets, the 145°F / 63°C rule and why fillets fall apart.

Fish is the best value in an air fryer: a fillet goes from cold to plated in under fifteen minutes, with a crisp edge instead of a soggy pan. It is also the protein most likely to come out in pieces.

Two things decide whether you get a whole fillet. Thickness, not weight, sets the clock. And the basket floor decides whether the fillet survives the flip.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Wattage and basket size change the clock, but 145°F / 63°C does not change.

The rule: 145°F / 63°C for fish#

FishSafe internal temperature
Cod, tilapia, haddock, catfish, all white fish145°F / 63°C
Salmon, tuna and other oily fish145°F / 63°C
Frozen breaded fish, cooked from frozen165°F / 74°C
Reheated fish leftovers165°F / 74°C

Fish carry no rest-time allowance and no ground-meat exception. The one number that surprises people is frozen breaded fish: the package product is a prepared food, so reheating it puts it under the 165°F / 74°C reheating standard rather than the fresh-fish number.

Probe the thickest part of the fillet, entering from the side rather than the top, because a thin fillet is shorter than the probe's sensing tip. If the reading looks low, move the probe and read again before you add time.

Air fryer fish chart by thickness#

Thickness is the only measurement that matters here. Two fillets can both weigh six ounces while one is half an inch thick and the other is nearly two inches. The thick one needs several more minutes, and the thin one is done before you expect it.

A general shortcut: one inch of fillet needs roughly ten minutes at 390–400°F / 199–205°C. Every additional half inch of thickness adds about three to four minutes, and the temperature should drop slightly as the fillet gets thicker.

Frozen breaded fillets and fish sticks#

Frozen breaded fish is the easiest fish to cook, because the coating is engineered to crisp and the product is usually par-cooked before freezing. It goes in from frozen, in a single layer, with no oil added.

Frozen breaded fishTemperatureTimeNotes
Fish sticks400°F / 205°C8–10 minShake at 5 min
Breaded fillets, thin400°F / 205°C10–12 minFlip at halfway
Breaded fillets, thick390°F / 199°C12–15 minSingle layer, no stack
Beer-battered fillets400°F / 205°C10–13 minBatter drips, use a liner
Panko-crusted fillets390°F / 199°C11–14 minPanko browns fast

Never stack frozen breaded fish. Two layers means the coating on the bottom fillets stays pale and steams while the top layer burns, and the pieces fuse together as they thaw.

Why fish falls apart in the basket#

Fish muscle is built in short flakes held together by thin connective sheets. Heat makes those sheets contract, and the flakes separate. Add a wet surface and a sticky basket and the fillet tears the moment you try to lift it. Four habits prevent it:

  1. Dry the fillet, then oil the fish and the basket. A dry surface browns and releases; a wet one glues itself down.
  2. Preheat for three minutes. A hot basket sets the bottom surface before the fillet can stick.
  3. Use a slotted spatula, not tongs. Tongs pinch a flaky fillet in half. Slide the spatula under and lift straight up.
  4. Flip once at most. Every extra touch is a chance to lose a corner.

If the fillet is very thin, lay it on a small piece of perforated parchment and lift the parchment out at the end. Do not use a solid sheet, and never let parchment touch the heating element.

Uneven fillets are the other failure. Fold the thin tail under the fillet so the whole piece is one thickness, and it will cook evenly instead of drying at one end while the other end finishes.

Five mistakes with air fryer fish#

  1. Cooking by the clock. A thin fillet goes from done to dry in about 90 seconds.
  2. A wet fillet. Surface water steams the fish and stops browning.
  3. Using weight instead of thickness. A thick six-ounce fillet and a thin six-ounce fillet need different times.
  4. Cranking the heat on a thick fillet. Above 400°F / 205°C the outside sets before the center is done.
  5. Adding oil to a breaded fillet. The coating already crisps, and oil makes it soft.

Frequently asked questions#

How long does fish take in an air fryer?

One inch of fresh white fish takes 9–14 minutes at 390–400°F / 199–205°C, depending on the species. Breaded frozen fillets take 10–15 minutes at 390–400°F / 199–205°C. Probe the thickest part and pull the fish at 145°F / 63°C.

Can I cook frozen fish fillets without thawing?

Yes, for breaded or par-cooked products, which are formulated to cook from frozen. Raw frozen fillets are harder to verify and cook unevenly at the edges. Thaw them in the refrigerator overnight, then use the fresh chart.

Should I flip fish in the air fryer?

A fillet an inch thick or less usually needs no flip. Thicker fillets benefit from one gentle turn at the halfway point, using a slotted spatula rather than tongs.

How do I know when fish is done without a thermometer?

You do not, reliably. Fish turns opaque, flakes separate when pressed, and the center loses its glassy look, but those are guesses that arrive late. A thermometer reading 145°F / 63°C is a measurement.

Is catfish different from other white fish?

It is firmer and holds together better, so it survives a flip more easily. Use the same temperature and add a minute or two over cod of the same thickness.

The short version#

Pat the fillet dry, oil it lightly, preheat the basket, and cook in a single layer at 390–400°F / 199–205°C for 7–14 minutes according to thickness. Lift it with a slotted spatula, turn it once at most, and pull it at 145°F / 63°C.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
