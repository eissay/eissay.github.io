Air Fryer Temperature Guide: When to Use 320, 350, 375 or 400

What each air fryer temperature is for, from 320°F / 160°C for delicate foods to 400°F / 205°C for crisping, with adjustments for frozen food.

Most air fryer confusion is temperature confusion. The machine can hold anything from 180°F / 82°C to 400°F / 205°C, and the difference between perfect and disappointing is usually just picking the right rung on that ladder — then leaving the food alone long enough.

This guide maps the common settings to the jobs they do best, explains why frozen food and fresh food rarely use the same number, and covers the adjustments that recipes assume you already know.

Times vary by appliance model. Wattage changes how fast a setting reaches the food, so use times as starting points and verify doneness the usual way.

What the air fryer actually does with temperature#

An air fryer is a compact convection oven: a heating element and a fan that pushes hot air around a small space. Because the chamber is small, the air moves fast and transfers heat more aggressively than a conventional oven at the same dial setting. That is why air fryer temperatures run 25°F / 14°C to 50°F / 28°C lower than the oven temperatures printed on frozen food bags.

The practical rule: when converting an oven recipe, subtract 25°F / 14°C and check for doneness about 20 percent earlier.

The temperature ladder#

SettingBest forExamples

340°F / 170°CGentle browning, glazes, vegetables that burn easilyOnions, sugary marinades
350°F / 177°CEven roasting, dense items, whole proteinsWhole chicken, meatloaf, thick bread
375°F / 190°CThe all-rounder for proteinsChicken breast, pork chops
380°F / 193°CStandard crisping with fat to renderThighs, wings, sausages
390°F / 199°CFaster crisping, thicker coatingsFrozen breaded foods
400°F / 205°CMaximum crisping, finishing, thin foodsFries, bacon, crisp finishes

Two settings anchor everything. Roasting dense food that must cook through uses moderate heat, 350°F / 177°C to 380°F / 193°C, so the center catches up before the surface burns. Crisping food that is already safe to eat — fries, frozen snacks, bacon — uses 400°F / 205°C, where speed and surface texture are the whole point.

Frozen versus fresh: why the numbers differ#

Frozen food usually wants a hotter setting than the same food fresh. The food starts cold in the middle, so the extra heat compensates while the coating still crisps.

FoodFreshFrozen
Chicken wings380°F / 193°C380°F / 193°C, longer time
Breaded chicken375°F / 190°C390°F / 199°C
French fries, from raw potato380°F / 193°C400°F / 205°C (bag instructions)
Fish fillets, breaded380°F / 193°C390°F / 199°C

The pattern to remember: freezing changes the clock more than the temperature, except for thin breaded items, where the coating benefits from the extra heat. Raw proteins are the exception to every shortcut here — thaw them first, because a frozen center makes doneness impossible to verify.

Preheating, shaking and other multipliers#

The dial is only one of three controls. The other two change results as much:

  • Preheating, 3 minutes. Food that starts in a hot basket browns sooner and sticks less. Most important for proteins and anything with a coating.
  • Shaking or flipping. Moving the food exposes new surfaces. Fries and small items want a shake every 5 to 8 minutes; larger cuts want one flip at halfway.
  • Basked density. A half-full basket cooks like the chart; a packed one needs longer and never crisps properly. Single layer is the rule the chart times assume.

Oil is the fourth variable, but it is forgiving: a light spray improves browning on most fresh foods, while pre-coated frozen food needs none.

When to lower the temperature instead of raising it#

Counterintuitively, most air fryer failures come from too much heat, not too little:

  • Burnt outside, cold inside. Drop 20°F / 11°C and add time; the heat needs to reach the center.
  • Sugary sauces turning black. Glaze in the last few minutes at a moderate setting.
  • Lean meat turning dry. Lower heat with a thermometer check beats high heat with a timer.
  • Cheese scorching on melts. Cover loosely with foil for the first half of the cook.

High heat is a finishing tool, not a cooking strategy. Cook through at a moderate setting, then raise the temperature for the last few minutes if you want extra color.

Five mistakes with air fryer temperatures#

  1. Following the oven temperature on a bag. Subtract 25°F / 14°C or the outside burns first.
  2. Using maximum heat for everything. 400°F / 205°C is for crisping, not for cooking through.
  3. Changing two variables at once. Adjust temperature or time, never both in the same batch.
  4. Skipping preheat for proteins. A cold start adds minutes unevenly and ruins timing.
  5. Trusting one machine's numbers blindly. Two models at the same setting can differ by 15°F / 8°C; learn yours.

Frequently asked questions#

What is the best all-purpose air fryer temperature?

375°F / 190°C to 380°F / 193°C covers most proteins and vegetables. If you only memorize one setting, make it 380°F / 193°C and adjust the time.

Why do frozen food instructions say to use a lower oven temperature?

They are written for still ovens. The air fryer's fast-moving air needs 25°F / 14°C less to deliver the same heat, and it cooks faster too.

Can I use 400°F / 205°C for raw chicken?

Not for the whole cook. Thin pieces might survive it, but on anything thick the surface scorches before the center is done. Cook at a moderate setting and finish hot if needed.

Does a bigger air fryer need different temperatures?

Larger chambers move more air over more food, so times stretch slightly and the temperature stays the same. Expect a few extra minutes versus the smallest baskets.

How do I know my machine runs hot or cool?

Watch the first few batches: consistent early browning means it runs hot, pale results that take longer than charts predict mean it runs cool. Adjust by 15°F / 8°C and note it.

Temperature is the steering wheel of the air fryer. Choose the rung that matches the food's density, verify doneness independently of the clock, and the same machine will handle delicate melts and hard crisps with equal confidence.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
