Air Fryer Chicken Thighs: Bone-In and Boneless Times

Air fryer chicken thighs time chart for bone-in skin-on and boneless cuts, with adjustments for size, skin and the internal temperature rule.

Chicken thighs are the most forgiving cut in the air fryer. More fat and more connective tissue mean the window between underdone and dry is wider than for breast — but the bone in many thighs slows the cook near the center, so the thermometer still decides.

This chart covers bone-in skin-on thighs, boneless skinless thighs and the adjustments that matter: size, skin, marinade and sauce. Whatever the style, the safe internal temperature for all poultry under USDA rules is 165°F / 74°C.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Wattage, basket size and crowding change the clock, but 165°F / 74°C does not change.

Air fryer chicken thighs chart#

Times assume a preheated basket, a single layer, and thighs of similar size. Skin-on thighs cook skin side up so the fat renders into the meat rather than the basket.

Thigh styleTemperatureTimeNotes
Bone-in skin-on, 5–7 oz380°F / 193°C25–30 minSkin side up, flip at halfway
Bone-in skin-on, 8–10 oz380°F / 193°C28–32 minCheck early, probe near bone
Boneless skinless, 4–6 oz380°F / 193°C18–22 minFlip at halfway
Boneless skinless, very large380°F / 193°C22–26 minPound thick ends flat
Marinated thighs380°F / 193°C25–30 minPat surface dry for browning

Probe bone-in thighs between the bone and the thickest muscle, not into the bone cavity — bone conducts heat differently and reads high. Boneless thighs probe the center of the thickest lobe.

Why thighs are safer to cook than breast#

Thigh meat stays juicy across a wider band of temperatures. Breast turns chalky when it climbs past 165°F / 74°C, but thighs keep improving as collagen softens well past that point. Many cooks take thighs to 175°F / 79°C or even 185°F / 85°C on purpose — the meat turns silkier, and since that is above the minimum, nothing about the safety rule changes.

Doneness stageInternal temperatureResult
USDA minimum for all poultry165°F / 74°CSafe, firmer texture
Collagen softening begins175°F / 79°CJuicier, more tender bite
Barbecue-style texture185°F / 85°CVery tender, shreddable

The one risk thighs carry is skin. Skin-on thighs need air on all sides; a thigh sitting in rendered fat steams instead of crisps. Do not stack them, and move the lower rack or basket position if your model browns unevenly.

From frozen or from the marinade#

Raw frozen thighs are the one shortcut to avoid. The outside overcooks while the center is still thawing, and the thermometer becomes unreliable when ice remains inside. Thaw in the refrigerator overnight — a 24 hour rest is not excessive for a thick thigh.

Marinated thighs go in wet, but wet surfaces brown poorly. Pat them with paper towel first, then add a thin layer of oil or the marinade's oily portion. Sugary marinades — teriyaki, honey, many bottled sauces — darken early; drop the temperature to 360°F / 182°C and add about 4–6 minutes to the time.

Five mistakes with air fryer chicken thighs#

  1. Cooking raw thighs from frozen. The center thaws while the outside dries; thaw overnight instead.
  2. Stacking thighs to save time. Air must reach every surface; overlap means steamed skin and uneven centers.
  3. Probing against the bone. Bone reads high; angle the thermometer into the thickest muscle instead.
  4. Pulling at exactly 165°F / 74°C every time. It is safe, but thighs are better at 175°F / 79°C or above.
  5. Bottling sugary sauce on from the start. Sauce burns at high heat; brush it on in the last 3–4 minutes.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do bone-in chicken thighs take in an air fryer?

25–30 minutes at 380°F / 193°C for a typical 5–7 ounce thigh, flipped halfway. Larger thighs need up to 32 minutes. Verify at the thickest part away from the bone.

What temperature should chicken thighs be inside?

All poultry must reach 165°F / 74°C under USDA rules. For a softer, more tender texture, cooking thighs to 175°F / 79°C or higher is common and stays above the safety minimum.

Do I flip chicken thighs in the air fryer?

Yes, at halfway. Skin-on thighs start skin side up; flipping once keeps browning even without drying the surface.

Can I use boneless thighs in the same chart?

Use the boneless row: 18–22 minutes at 380°F / 193°C for 4–6 ounce thighs. Boneless cooks faster because heat does not detour around a bone.

Thighs forgive small timing errors better than any other cut. Keep the layer single, probe away from the bone, and treat 165°F / 74°C as the floor rather than the finish line — the result is crisp skin and meat that shreds under a fork.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
