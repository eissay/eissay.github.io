Air Fryer Stuffed Peppers: Times and Temperatures

A stuffed pepper is two foods cooking at once: a pepper wall that softens fast and a filling that lags behind. The air fryer handles that split well — hot air browns the top of the filling while the pepper turns tender — but only if you give the filling the time its meat requires.

This chart covers raw-meat fillings, pre-cooked and vegetarian versions, plus the make-ahead steps that turn stuffed peppers into a meal-prep workhorse.

Times vary by appliance model. Pepper size, filling density and starting temperature all move the clock — check the first batch and adjust.

The rule that decides everything#

The meat inside sets the schedule. A filling with raw ground beef, pork or sausage must reach 160°F / 71°C in the center — the USDA rule for ground meat — and it is measured in the middle of the filling, not the pepper wall. Ground chicken or turkey fillings follow the poultry rule instead: 165°F / 74°C. Fillings that are already cooked — beans, rice, roasted vegetables, cheese — need no meat temperature at all, just a hot center.

If the top browns before the center is done, lay a small piece of foil over the peppers and keep going. The foil blocks the browning, not the heat, and buys the filling the extra minutes it needs.

Peppers need headroom and balance: fill each one to three-quarters, not the brim, and keep the pieces upright in the basket. A ramekin under each pepper, a crumpled foil ring around the base, or a thin slice trimmed off the bottom to make a flat foot all stop the tipping. Two peppers fit most baskets — cook in batches rather than stacking.

The pepper wall reads done long before the filling does, so never judge by how the pepper looks or feels. Push an instant-read thermometer into the middle of the filling, hold it there for a few seconds until the number stops climbing, and compare it to the table. If it falls short, give the peppers 3–5 more minutes and measure again.

There is no rest requirement for stuffed peppers — ground meat has no rest rule, and the peppers hold heat well on the plate. Serve them straight from the basket.

Make-ahead and meal prep#

Stuffed peppers survive every make-ahead step without complaint, which is why they earn a permanent slot in a meal-prep rotation.

StepWhenHow

Leftovers follow the reheating rule: 165°F / 74°C in the center, whether the filling has meat or not. For batch prep, cook a full basket, cool the extras within two hours, and box them — the fridge batch reheats in minutes and the freezer batch is a ready-made dinner.

Five mistakes with air fryer stuffed peppers#

  1. Judging done by the pepper, not the filling. The wall softens in 10 minutes; only 160°F / 71°C in the middle of a meat filling means done.
  2. Overfilling the peppers. Rice and meat expand past the brim, spill onto the basket and burn; fill to three-quarters.
  3. Skipping the foil tent. A top that browns at minute 8 with a cold center needs foil for the rest of the cook, not surrender.
  4. Letting them tip over. A pepper on its side cooks unevenly and leaks; ramekins, foil rings or a trimmed base keep them upright.
  5. Reheating on the fresh-cook clock. Cold peppers from the fridge need 3–5 extra minutes; from frozen, start at 15–20 min and verify the center.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do stuffed peppers take in the air fryer?

15–20 minutes at 370°F / 188°C for a raw meat filling with rice, 10–12 minutes for vegetarian fillings, and 12–15 minutes for a chilled pre-cooked filling. Verify the center with a thermometer on the first batch.

Do I need to soften the peppers before stuffing?

No — the full cook softens them. For very thick-walled peppers you can pre-soften plain halves for 8–10 minutes at 380°F / 193°C, then stuff and finish at 370°F / 188°C for 10–12 minutes.

Can I use uncooked rice in the filling?

Use cooked rice. Uncooked rice needs liquid and a long simmer that 15–20 minutes of hot air cannot provide; it stays crunchy while the pepper overcooks around it.

How do I know the filling is done without cutting a pepper open?

A thermometer in the center: 160°F / 71°C for ground beef, pork or sausage, and 165°F / 74°C for ground poultry or any chilled cooked filling.

Can I freeze stuffed peppers?

Freeze them after cooking: cool, wrap each pepper, and keep for 2–3 months. Reheat from frozen at 370°F / 188°C for 15–20 minutes with foil over the top for the first half, until the center reads 165°F / 74°C.

Stuffed peppers reward one habit: measure the filling, not the clock. 370°F / 188°C, 15–20 minutes, and a thermometer reading in the center turn out the same batch every time — which is exactly what you want from a meal-prep staple.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
