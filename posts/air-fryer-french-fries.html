Air Fryer French Fries: Fresh-Cut and Frozen Times

French fries are the number-one reason people buy an air fryer — and the first thing that disappoints them, because fries are the most technique-sensitive item in the basket. Get the moisture out, give them room, and shake often: those three rules turn out fries that crackle, whether you cut them yourself or open a freezer bag.

This chart covers fresh-cut fries with the double-cook method, plus frozen fries by style.

Times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and fry thickness. Frozen fry styles differ — check the package direction and adjust from the ranges below.

Doneness is color and sound, not a thermometer: deep gold with hard, crackly edges. A pale fry with bend is not done — give it two more minutes and check again rather than trusting the timer.

Fresh-cut fries: the soak-and-dry method#

Fresh potatoes carry surface starch and water, and both turn to steam in a hot basket. The method removes them in order:

StepTimeWhy
Cut sticks, rinse once—Washes off loose surface starch
Soak in cold water30 minPulls starch from inside the stick
Dry thoroughly, towel2 minWet sticks steam — this is the step people skip
Toss with 1 tbsp oil per large potato1 minEven browning without sogginess
Stage 1 at 380°F / 193°C8–10 minCooks the inside
Rest 5 min5 minSteam escapes; center firms
Stage 2 at 400°F / 205°C5–7 minBuilds the crust

The rest between stages matters more than it looks: a fry that goes straight from stage one to high heat browns before its center sets. Five minutes on the counter, then the hot finish, is what separates air fryer fries from soft oven fries.

Salt the fries the moment they leave the basket — on hot, freshly oiled surfaces the salt sticks; on cooled ones it rolls off.

Frozen fries: do less, not more#

Frozen fries are blanched and pre-fried at the factory, so your only job is to finish them dry and hot. Do not add oil — the coating has enough, and extra oil makes them greasy instead of crisp. Do not thaw them — they cook from frozen or they turn limp.

Shake twice, at a third and two-thirds through the time. Shoestring fries move fast at 400°F / 205°C: from golden to dark in about two minutes, so start checking at minute nine.

The air fryer's real advantage shows on small batches: four servings crisp in the basket while the oven would need fifteen minutes to preheat for the same result.

Five mistakes with air fryer french fries#

  1. Skipping the dry step on fresh-cut. Surface water steams the stick from the outside in; towel-dry until nothing glistens.
  2. Piling fries above the max line. Fries need air on every side — cook in two batches rather than filling the basket.
  3. Ignoring the shake. The layer touching the basket browns first; a shake every five to six minutes evens it out.
  4. Adding oil to frozen fries. They arrive pre-fried; extra oil pools and softens the coating.
  5. Salting too late or too early. Salt right out of the basket — hot and dry is when it clings.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do frozen fries take in an air fryer?

12–16 minutes at 400°F / 205°C for straight-cut, 10–13 for shoestring and 16–20 for steak-cut styles, shaking twice. No oil, no thawing.

Why are my air fryer fries soggy?

Usually moisture: undried fresh-cut sticks, crowded baskets, or a shake skipped. Dry the sticks fully, spread them in one layer, and shake every five to six minutes.

Should I soak fresh fries before air frying?

A 30-minute cold-water soak pulls out starch and helps even browning. It helps, but the step that matters more is drying them completely afterward.

Can I reuse the oil I tossed the fresh fries in?

For the same batch, yes — any leftover tossed oil has nowhere to go anyway. For storage, oil that touched raw potato water is fine; just strain it if you plan to use it within a day or two.

How do I reheat leftover fries?

400°F / 205°C for 3–4 minutes in a single layer. Leftover cooked foods should reach 165°F / 74°C when reheated — for fries that is usually a non-issue, but the hot basket restores the crunch either way.

Fresh-cut or frozen, fries reward the same discipline: dry surfaces, open air, frequent shakes. Master that and the air fryer earns its counter space on this one dish alone.
