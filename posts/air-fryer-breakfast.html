Air Fryer Breakfast: Times and Temperatures for the First Meal

Air fryer breakfast times for hash browns, sausage, egg bites, French toast sticks, pancakes, bagels and a hot breakfast sandwich.

Breakfast is where an air fryer earns its counter space. Frozen hash browns, sausage links and French toast sticks come out crisp in the time it takes to set the table, and none of them need a pan of oil.

The hard part is the order, because one basket and one temperature cannot do everything at once.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer for the meat items, and treat every time below as a range. Wattage and basket size move the clock by several minutes.

The breakfast chart#

Times assume a preheated basket and a single layer. Flip or shake at the halfway mark unless the notes say otherwise.

ItemTemperatureTimeNotes
Frozen hash brown patties400°F / 205°C10–14 minFlip at halfway
Shredded hash browns, fresh380°F / 193°C12–16 minPress into a flat layer
Frozen sausage links380°F / 193°C10–14 minShake at 6 min
Breakfast sausage patties, raw pork380°F / 193°C10–14 min160°F / 71°C
Chicken or turkey breakfast patties380°F / 193°C11–15 min165°F / 74°C
Egg bites in a silicone mold300°F / 149°C12–16 minCenter just set
French toast sticks, frozen380°F / 193°C8–11 minShake at 5 min
Frozen pancakes350°F / 177°C6–9 minFlip once
Bagel halves375°F / 190°C3–5 minCut side up

Two items deserve a note. Hash browns want the highest setting because they are thin and full of surface water that must leave before they brown. Egg bites want the lowest, because high heat puffs the outside and leaves the middle liquid.

Timing several items in one basket#

You cannot cook everything at once, but two or three items can share a basket. Start with the item that needs the longest time, then add the faster items partway through.

CombinationTemperatureHow to time it
Hash brown patties and sausage links380°F / 193°CStart the hash browns, add the links at 6 min
French toast sticks and sausage links380°F / 193°CStart the links, add the sticks at 5 min
Bagel halves and a sausage patty375°F / 190°CCook the patty first, toast the bagel in the last 4 min

If two items want different temperatures, cook them in separate batches. Egg bites at 300°F / 149°C cannot share a basket with anything else on this list.

Never stack. A second layer slows the whole basket and the food underneath steams. If the basket is full, add 2–4 minutes.

Building a breakfast sandwich#

Assembly is a sequence, not a recipe. Cook the parts, then stack them while everything is hot.

StepTemperatureTime
Toast bagel halves375°F / 190°C3–5 min
Cook or reheat the sausage patty380°F / 193°C10–14 min
Set an egg in a small oiled ramekin320°F / 160°C9–13 min
Melt the cheese on the open sandwich350°F / 177°C2–3 min

Choose a bagel or muffin that sits flat, put the cheese on the top half, and return the open sandwich for the last two minutes so the cheese melts without steaming the bread. A closed sandwich wrapped in foil traps steam, and loose foil is also a fire risk if it lifts into the element.

Reheating cooked breakfast meat#

Cooked sausage, patties or any breakfast meat that spent the night in the refrigerator goes back into the basket at 350°F / 177°C for 4–6 minutes, and it must reach 165°F / 74°C. That is the USDA figure for reheated leftovers, and it is higher than the temperature the sausage reached the first time. Reheating is about getting the whole piece hot enough to be safe again, not about cooking it further. Probe the middle of the thickest piece.

Five mistakes with air fryer breakfast#

  1. Stacking items to fit them all in. Two layers steam the bottom row and slow everything.
  2. Using one temperature for everything. Egg bites at 380°F / 193°C set at the edges and stay liquid in the middle.
  3. Cooking the fastest item first. Sausage takes twice as long as a bagel, so start with the long one.
  4. Judging sausage by color. A browned patty can still be well short of its target temperature.
  5. Reheating at 300°F / 149°C. The meat warms but does not reach 165°F / 74°C in a useful time.

Frequently asked questions#

What temperature do I cook breakfast sausage in an air fryer? Raw pork patties and links are 380°F / 193°C for 10–14 minutes, cooked to 160°F / 71°C. Chicken or turkey patties need 165°F / 74°C. Fully cooked smoked sausage only needs heating.

Can I cook eggs in an air fryer? Yes, in a small oiled ramekin or a silicone mold, at 320°F / 160°C for 9–13 minutes, until the center is just set. USDA puts egg dishes at 160°F / 71°C, the same target read on a thermometer.

How long do frozen hash browns take? 10–14 minutes at 400°F / 205°C for patties, flipped at halfway. Loose shredded hash browns need 12–16 minutes and a flat, pressed layer.

Can I cook bacon in the air fryer? Yes, but it is a different job with different rules. Bacon has its own chart on this site, so this page does not repeat it.

The short version#

Preheat the basket, keep one layer, and group items by temperature: 380°F / 193°C covers sausage, hash browns and French toast sticks, egg bites want 300°F / 149°C, and bagel halves need only 3–5 minutes at 375°F / 190°C. Reheat cooked breakfast meat to 165°F / 74°C, and trust a thermometer rather than the color.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
