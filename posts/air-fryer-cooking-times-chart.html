Air Fryer Cooking Times Chart (Free Printable)

One printable air fryer cooking times chart covering frozen foods, vegetables, chicken, fish and reheating — with the temperature rule that keeps food safe.

Air fryers cook fast because they move hot air hard. That same speed is why the first attempt at a new food usually goes wrong: a minute or two is the difference between crisp and dry.

This is the chart I keep on the fridge. Use it as a starting point, then trust your own machine for the final minute.

The one rule that matters more than any time on this chart

Air fryer models differ hugely in wattage, basket size and how close the heating element sits to the food. A time that works in a 5.8-quart basket can overcook in a 2-quart one.

So the chart gives ranges, not commands, and the real check is temperature:

Five mistakes that ruin air fryer food

  1. Overcrowding the basket. Air has to circulate. Two layers means steaming, not frying.
  2. Skipping the preheat. Three minutes of preheat is the difference between crisp and pale.
  3. Too little oil on vegetables. A dry surface burns before the inside cooks.
  4. Not shaking. Every 8–10 minutes for anything in a pile.
  5. Going by the clock instead of a thermometer. Especially with chicken.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to preheat an air fryer? For anything you want browned, yes — about 3 minutes at the cooking temperature. For reheating and for delicate items, it matters less.

Should I line the basket with foil or parchment? Only if the liner is perforated or the food sits above it. A solid liner blocks the airflow the machine depends on and can be a fire risk if it lifts into the element.

Is an air fryer the same as a convection oven? Functionally close: a small, fast convection oven. The difference is proximity — the element is inches from the food, which is why times are shorter.
