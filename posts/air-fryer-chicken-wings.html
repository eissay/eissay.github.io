Air Fryer Chicken Wings: Time and Temperature Chart

Air fryer chicken wings time and temperature chart for fresh and frozen wings, with sauce timing, crisp finishes and the 165°F / 74°C rule.

Wings are the food the air fryer was practically made for: circulating hot air crisps the skin all over, with far less oil than deep frying and no oven splatter. The catch is that wings carry a lot of skin and fat, so they can look glossy and done while the joint is still undercooked.

This chart gives starting times for fresh and frozen wings, plus the timing that keeps sauce from burning. The number that ends the cook is not on this page — it is what your thermometer reads in the thickest part of the drumette.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Wattage, basket size and crowding change the clock, but 165°F / 74°C does not change.

The rule that matters most for wings#

Wings are poultry, and poultry has one internal temperature under USDA rules. There is no sauce, rub or cooking style that changes it:

PoultrySafe internal temperature
Fresh or thawed chicken wings165°F / 74°C
Frozen chicken wings, cooked from frozen165°F / 74°C
Breaded chicken wings, fresh or frozen165°F / 74°C
Cooked wings reheated later165°F / 74°C

Probe the thickest part of the drumette, keeping the thermometer tip away from the bone. If the largest wing reads 165°F / 74°C, the smaller ones around it are done too.

Air fryer chicken wings chart: fresh#

Times assume a preheated basket, a single layer and wings of similar size. Shake the basket every 8 minutes so the skin renders evenly.

Wing styleTemperatureTimeNotes
Split wings, drums and flats380°F / 193°C22–26 minShake every 8 minutes
Whole wings, tips attached380°F / 193°C24–28 minFlip at halfway
Dry-rubbed wings380°F / 193°C22–26 minRubs with sugar darken faster
Breaded fresh wings380°F / 193°C20–24 minSingle layer, light oil spray
Cornstarch-dusted wings380°F / 193°C22–26 minShake once more than usual

Start checking at the 20 minute mark. A range tells you when to begin checking, not when to stop. Wings hold heat well, so a 3 minute rest before saucing keeps them hot and juicy.

Crispy finish and sauce timing#

The order of sauce and heat decides whether wings end crisp or soggy. The safest sequence: cook fully, sauce hot, serve immediately. If you want a lacquered glaze, use the short finish in the table below.

StepTemperatureTimeNotes
Extra-crisp finish, plain wings400°F / 205°C3–5 minAfter the main cook, watch closely
Glaze during the last minutes380°F / 193°C2–3 minThin glaze only; sugars burn fast
Buffalo sauce after cookingOff heatToss while hotBest crust, no burning risk
Butter-based sauce after cookingOff heatToss and serveSit too long and skin softens

A full buffalo treatment — sauce during cooking at high heat — is the most common way wings burn in an air fryer. The sauce contains sugar and butter, and both scorch above 400°F / 205°C. Sauce after the cook, or glaze for only the last 2–3 minutes.

Air fryer chicken wings chart: frozen#

Frozen wings cook well from frozen because the coating is designed for it. No thawing, no oil, no rinsing.

Frozen wingsTemperatureTimeNotes
Breaded frozen wings380°F / 193°C20–24 minShake every 8 minutes
Unbreaded frozen wings380°F / 193°C24–28 minShake twice during the cook
Frozen glazed wings380°F / 193°C20–24 minFoil under the basket catches drips

Because frozen wings start cold in the center, the thermometer matters even more. Breaded frozen wings frequently need the full range before the thickest drumette reaches 165°F / 74°C.

Five mistakes with air fryer wings#

  1. Saucing before the skin crisps. Wet sauce on raw skin steams it; sauce after cooking keeps the crust.
  2. Crowding the basket. Two layers means steamed wings in the middle and burnt wings on the edge.
  3. Judging by color. Skin can be deep golden while the joint is undercooked; only the thermometer knows.
  4. Skipping the shakes. Wings sitting in rendered fat cook unevenly — shake every 8 minutes.
  5. Cranking past 400°F / 205°C. Fat spatters onto the element and the skin burns before the collagen softens.

Frequently asked questions#

Can wings be pink near the bone and still be done?

Yes. Bone marrow can tint the meat pink even when the wing is fully cooked. Wings are safe when the thickest part of the drumette reaches 165°F / 74°C, regardless of color.

Do wings need oil in the air fryer?

Plain wings benefit from a light spray for even browning; breaded wings need none, since the coating already contains fat.

How do I keep buffalo sauce from burning?

Toss the wings in sauce after cooking, while they are hot. For a baked-on glaze, brush a thin layer and hold at 380°F / 193°C for only 2–3 minutes.

Why are my wings tough instead of crisp?

The heat is too high for too long. Skin crisps between 380°F / 193°C and 400°F / 205°C; the collagen in the joint needs time at temperature, not more temperature.

Can I cook then hold wings for a party?

Cook fully, rest 5 minutes, then hold in a 200°F / 93°C oven or warm setting for up to 30 minutes. Sauce at serving time, not before.

Wings reward patience more than power. Cook at 380°F / 193°C, shake often, verify with a thermometer at the thickest joint, and sauce at the end — that sequence gives crisp skin and a safe, juicy interior every time.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
