Air Fryer Tofu: Crispy Cubes, Times and Temperatures

Air fryer tofu times and temperatures by cube size, plus pressing, firm versus extra-firm versus silken tofu, cornstarch coating and a crispiness fix.

Tofu is the one air fryer food where preparation decides everything. A wet, bare block comes out pale, soft, and stuck to the plate. The same block, pressed and dusted with cornstarch, comes out with a shell you can hear when you bite it.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer for the numbers that matter, and treat every time below as a range. Wattage and basket size can move the clock by several minutes.

Tofu is ready to eat, so there is no USDA minimum temperature

This is the honest version. USDA publishes safe minimum internal temperatures for meat, poultry, fish and egg dishes. It does not publish one for tofu, because tofu is not a raw animal product and does not need to reach a safety temperature to be eaten. So there is no tofu temperature to hit, and this chart will not invent one. The temperatures below are cooking settings for browning and texture; the numbers that matter here are about storage and reheating.

Firm, extra-firm and silken: only two of these work

Tofu is sold by water content, and water is the enemy of crispness.

Tofu typeWaterAir fryer resultVerdict
SilkenVery highSteams inside, never browns, falls apartSkip it
SoftHighBreaks apart when tossedSkip it
FirmMediumBrowns, soft center, needs longer pressingWorks
Extra-firmLowCrisp edges, holds its shapeBest choice
Super-firm or pressedLowestVery crisp, almost chewyWorks, watch the time

Silken tofu fails for a physical reason. It is set with less coagulant and holds far more water, so in hot moving air that water turns to steam inside the cube. The surface never stays dry long enough to brown. Silken belongs in soup; firm and extra-firm are the ones that crisp.

Pressing: fifteen minutes minimum

Wrap the block in a clean towel, set it on a plate, and put a heavy pan or a couple of cans on top for 15–30 minutes. That removes the water that would otherwise steam the cube from the inside.

Short on time? Cut the block into cubes first, spread them on a towel, and press a second towel over them for 10 minutes. Salt after pressing, not before: salt early draws out more water and leaves the surface damp.

Cornstarch: the coating that makes the crust

Dust, do not dredge. One tablespoon of cornstarch for a 14–16 oz block, tossed until every face has a thin coat, then shake off the excess. A thick layer turns gummy and tastes of raw starch.

A teaspoon of oil in the bowl helps the coating stick and brown. Skip wet batter: it drips and can burn onto the element.

Flipping: shake, do not fuss

A single layer is worth more than any amount of flipping. When cubes touch, those faces stay pale.

Shake the basket every 5–6 minutes for half-inch and three-quarter-inch cubes, and roll triangles and one-inch cubes with tongs once at halfway. Do not open the basket constantly; the temperature drops every time.

Five mistakes with air fryer tofu

  1. Skipping the press. Wet tofu steams instead of browning, and no setting fixes that.
  2. Buying silken for a crisp recipe. It cannot hold its shape in moving air.
  3. Crowding the basket. Two layers cook unevenly and the middle stays soft.
  4. Saucing too early. A wet sauce softens the crust you just built.
  5. Expecting deep-fried crunch with no oil. A light oil toss gets close; none at all gets dry.

Frequently asked questions

What temperature do I cook tofu in an air fryer? Between 380°F / 193°C and 400°F / 205°C for most cubes. Half-inch cubes take the higher setting; one-inch cubes brown more evenly at the lower one.

How long does tofu take in an air fryer? 10–13 minutes for half-inch cubes and 14–18 minutes for one-inch cubes, shaken at halfway.

Is reheated tofu safe? Yes. Reheat leftovers to 165°F / 74°C, the USDA figure for reheated food, and eat cooked tofu within 3–4 days.

The short version

Press, cut evenly, dust with cornstarch, oil lightly, preheat, and cook in a single layer at 380°F / 193°C to 400°F / 205°C for 10–18 minutes depending on size. Shake at halfway and sauce after the basket comes out. There is no internal temperature to hit.
