How to Reheat Leftovers in an Air Fryer (Chart and Times)

Reheat leftovers in an air fryer: pizza, fried chicken, fries, egg rolls and more, with times, temperatures and the 165°F / 74°C rule.

Reheating is the job an air fryer does better than anything else in the kitchen. Last night's pizza comes back with a crisp base instead of a chewy one, and fried chicken loses that damp coating.

The reason is mechanical, not magical: hot moving air removes surface moisture and browns the outside, which is exactly what a microwave cannot do.

Air fryer models differ in wattage, basket size and how close the element sits to the food. Treat every time below as a range, and verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer.

Why the microwave disappoints and the air fryer does not#

A microwave heats food by agitating water molecules. The energy goes into the water inside the food, and the surface never gets hot and dry enough to brown. The differences follow from that:

What happensMicrowaveAir fryer
Heat sourceWater molecules absorb microwavesHot air at 350–400°F / 177–205°C
SurfaceStays damp and softDries out and browns
SteamTrapped inside the containerEscapes through the basket
Heating patternUneven, with hot and cold spotsEven, from moving air
Bread and pizza crustChewy or leatheryCrisp again in 3–5 min
Breading and batterTurns limpRe-crisps rather than softening

A covered microwave container traps the steam that a fried coating needs to lose. The air fryer does the opposite: the air is well above the boiling point of water, so moisture leaves the surface and browning begins. That is why a 3–5 min pass brings fries and egg rolls back.

It also cannot do everything. Anything with a lot of sauce or liquid will dry out, spit, or burn at the edges before the center is hot. Soups and stews belong in a saucepan.

The one number that matters: 165°F / 74°C#

The widely used guidance is that reheated leftovers should reach 165°F / 74°C throughout, measured in the thickest part with an instant-read thermometer. A microwave can leave cold spots; an air fryer is more even, but the thermometer is still the check.

FoodSafe internal temperature
Leftovers, reheated165°F / 74°C
Chicken and turkey (all parts)165°F / 74°C
Ground meat (beef, pork, lamb)160°F / 71°C
Whole cuts of beef, pork, veal145°F / 63°C + 3 min rest
Fish and shellfish145°F / 63°C

If you are reheating a mixed plate, go by the strictest item on it — usually the chicken.

Rice needs a plain warning#

Cooked rice is not like other leftovers. It can carry spores that survive cooking, and left at room temperature those spores can multiply and produce a toxin that reheating does not destroy.

So: refrigerate cooked rice promptly, do not leave it sitting out at room temperature, and do not reheat it more than once. When you do reheat it, take it to 165°F / 74°C. If rice has been out at room temperature for a long stretch, throw it away rather than reheating it.

The common guidance for cooked leftovers in the refrigerator is 3–4 days. Follow your local food-safety authority if it gives different numbers.

Frequently asked questions#

Can I reheat leftovers in the air fryer straight from frozen?

Yes for breaded and baked items, but add time and expect a wider range. Pizza, fries and casseroles work; anything raw in the middle does not, so check with a thermometer.

Should I preheat for reheating?

For pizza, fries and fried chicken, a 3–5 min preheat gives a better crust. For rice, casseroles and vegetables, it matters less.

Do I need oil?

Usually no. A light spray on fries or breaded food helps browning, but most leftovers already carry enough fat.

Can I reheat on parchment in the basket?

Only if the liner is perforated and weighted down by the food. A loose liner can lift into the element.

Why did my casserole dry out?

Because the air is hot and dry. Add a small splash of water and tent loosely with foil for the first part of the reheat.

Five mistakes that ruin reheated food#

  1. Going by the clock. Model wattage and basket size vary more than people expect.
  2. Piling food in. One layer or the middle stays cold and the outside dries.
  3. Reheating rice carelessly. Cool it fast, refrigerate it, reheat it once, and take it to 165°F / 74°C.
  4. Trying soup in the air fryer. Liquids splash into the element and nothing browns.
  5. Skipping the thermometer on mixed plates. Chicken, rice and casseroles all need 165°F / 74°C.

The short version#

Pizza, fried chicken, fries, egg rolls and roasted vegetables come back crisp in 3–12 min because hot moving air dries and browns the surface that a microwave leaves damp. Heat to 165°F / 74°C, keep rice on a short leash, and use a saucepan for anything wet.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
