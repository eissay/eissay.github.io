Air Fryer Sausage: Brats, Italian and Breakfast Times

Air fryer sausage times for fresh bratwurst and Italian links, smoked sausages and breakfast patties, with the internal temperatures to verify.

Sausages were born for hot air: the casing browns, the fat renders into the tray instead of the flame, and there is no flare-up to manage. But the sausage aisle mixes two very different products — raw links that must be cooked through, and fully cooked sausages that only need heating — and cooking them the same way ruins one of them.

This chart splits the two categories and gives times for each. Fresh sausage is ground pork, so under USDA rules it must reach 160°F / 71°C inside; fully cooked sausage just needs to be heated through.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Wattage, basket size and link size change the clock, but the internal temperature rule does not change.

The two categories that matter#

Read the package. The words "fully cooked", "smoked" or "heat and serve" mean the sausage is safe already and only needs browning. Anything labeled "raw" or "cook thoroughly" — most bratwurst, Italian links and breakfast patties — is ground meat and must be verified.

Sausage typeStatusTarget internal temperature
Fresh bratwurst, Italian, breakfast linksRaw160°F / 71°C
Breakfast sausage pattiesRaw160°F / 71°C
Smoked sausage, kielbasa, andouilleFully cookedHeat until steaming hot
Hot dogs and wienersFully cookedHeat until steaming hot

For raw links, probe the center of the thickest link at an angle, keeping the tip in the meat and away from the casing. Fully cooked sausages need no probe — heat them until the center is hot to the bite.

Air fryer sausage chart#

Times assume a preheated basket and a single layer. Do not pierce the casings — the juices that keep the sausage juicy escape through every hole.

Fresh links benefit from a brief lower-temperature start — 3 minutes at 340°F / 170°C before raising to 380°F / 193°C — when the casings are thick and you want zero split skins. It is optional; the straight 380°F / 193°C cook is the standard.

Beer, onions and the rest of the bratwurst ritual#

Traditional bratwurst is simmered in beer and onions before grilling. In the air fryer the par-simmer still works: poach the raw links gently in simmering beer or broth for 8–10 minutes, then brown them in the basket for 8–10 minutes at 380°F / 193°C. The poach keeps the interior juicy while the basket supplies the crust.

Skip the poach and simply extend the basket time to the long end of the range — 15 minutes — turning twice. Either route ends the same way: the thickest link probes 160°F / 71°C before it comes out.

Caramelized onions cook beautifully in the basket after the sausages come out: thin-sliced onions, a spoon of butter, 340°F / 170°C for 12–15 minutes with one stir at halfway.

Five mistakes with air fryer sausage#

  1. Piercing the casing. Every fork hole drains the juices; tongs and a probe in the center instead.
  2. Cooking raw and smoked sausages the same. Raw needs the full time and a probe; smoked only needs browning.
  3. Stacking links in a second row. Overlap blocks the air and steams the covered casing.
  4. Maxing the heat for speed. Past 400°F / 205°C the casing splits and burns before the center cooks.
  5. Serving straight from the basket without checking. Fresh sausage must probe 160°F / 71°C, every batch.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do bratwurst take in an air fryer?

12–15 minutes at 380°F / 193°C for raw brats, turned at halfway, until the center probes 160°F / 71°C. Fully cooked brats need only 8–10 minutes to brown.

Do I need to boil brats before air frying?

No. A short beer or broth poach is optional and adds juiciness; the basket alone at the long end of the range does the job.

What temperature does sausage need inside?

Fresh sausage is ground pork and must reach 160°F / 71°C under USDA rules. Fully cooked sausages have no raw center to worry about — heat them until steaming.

Why did my casings split?

Heat too high or casings pierced. Keep the basket at 380°F / 193°C or below and handle the links with tongs, never a fork.

Can I cook frozen sausages in the air fryer?

Fully cooked frozen sausages: yes, add 3–5 minutes to the browning time. Raw frozen links: thaw first, because the center must be verified and ice ruins the reading.

Two numbers carry this whole page: 160°F / 71°C for anything raw, and browning-only for anything fully cooked. Read the package, keep the layer single, and the air fryer turns out sausages better than any grill.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
