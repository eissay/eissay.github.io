Air fryer pizza times and temperatures for frozen personal pizza, leftover slices, fresh dough, tortilla, naan and pita bases, with checks for each.

Pizza is one of the best things an air fryer does, as long as you match the base to the basket. A tortilla turns crisp in five minutes. Raw dough in the same basket at the same temperature comes out pale and doughy.

The difference is water and mass. Thin, dry bases lose their moisture fast and brown. Fresh dough carries a lot of water and needs room and time to cook through before the top burns.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Frozen pizzas and reheated leftovers should reach 165°F / 74°C in the centre before you eat them.

Frozen, leftover and raw are three different jobs#

Frozen personal pizza. It goes in cold, straight from the freezer, and the centre is the last thing to heat. The crust browns before the middle is hot, which is why the range is wide. Probe the centre of the cheese and sauce, not the crust, and look for 165°F / 74°C. Some thin frozen pizzas finish in 8 minutes; a thick one with dense toppings can take 12.

Leftover slices. This is a short reheat, not a bake. Three to five minutes at 360°F / 182°C brings back the crisp base that a microwave destroys. Put the slice cheese side up, keep it in a single layer, and take it out as soon as the cheese loosens. Reheated leftovers should reach 165°F / 74°C, so if the slice is thick, give it another minute rather than eating it lukewarm. Refrigerate leftover pizza within two hours of cooking and eat it within 3–4 days.

Fresh dough. The one case that genuinely needs a different method. See below.

Why wet dough needs a smaller basket and lower heat#

Fresh pizza dough is roughly 60 percent water, and that water has to leave the dough before the base can crisp. In a small basket there is less air volume, so the fan recirculates hot air over a shorter path and the element can sit much closer to the top of the dough. That is a problem: the cheese and the crust surface brown and burn while the inside of the dough is still raw and wet.

Two adjustments fix it:

  • Use a smaller base, not a bigger oven. Roll the dough to a size that leaves clearance around it. A 6-inch round in a 6-quart basket cooks far better than an 8-inch round pressed against the sides, because air has to be able to move around the edge and under the base.
  • Drop the temperature to 350–375°F / 177–190°C. Lower air temperature buys the inside of the dough time to set before the top is dark. If your cheese is browning before the base is firm, drop 25°F / 15°C.

A useful trick is a two-stage bake. Put the rolled dough in alone for 4–5 minutes at 350°F / 177°C, flip it, then add sauce and cheese and finish for another 3–5 minutes. The part-bake firms the base so it can support the toppings.

Five mistakes with air fryer pizza#

  1. Not preheating. A cold basket gives you a soggy bottom. Give it 3–5 minutes.
  2. Using a base that touches the sides. Air cannot circulate, so the edge stays pale and the middle cooks unevenly.
  3. Piling on wet toppings. Every watery topping adds steam that keeps the crust soft.
  4. Cooking raw dough at 400°F / 205°C. The top burns before the dough sets. Lower the heat and part-bake the base.
  5. Leaving the pizza in the basket after it is done. The residual heat and trapped steam soften the base within a couple of minutes. Move it to a rack.

Frequently asked questions#

Can I cook a frozen pizza in an air fryer? Yes, if it fits flat with clearance around it. Cook at 370–390°F / 188–199°C for 8–12 min and check the centre for 165°F / 74°C. Cut a larger pizza to fit rather than bending it.

How long do you reheat pizza in an air fryer? 3–5 min at 360°F / 182°C for a standard slice. Two slices side by side need 1–2 min more, and they should not overlap.

Can I put raw pizza dough in an air fryer? Yes, rolled thin and no wider than the basket floor. Part-bake the base for 4–5 min at 350°F / 177°C, then add toppings and finish for 3–5 min.

Do I need parchment under pizza? It helps with cleanup on fresh dough, but it must be perforated and weighted down by the dough. A loose liner can lift into the heating element, so never use one you cannot pin under the food.

Is a tortilla pizza the same as a flatbread pizza? Close enough. Both are thin, dry bases that cook in 4–7 min at 375–400°F / 190–205°C. The tortilla is thinner, so it needs the shorter time and more attention.

The short version#

Match the base to the job. Frozen personal pizzas take 8–12 min at 370–390°F / 188–199°C. Leftover slices take 3–5 min at 360°F / 182°C. Fresh dough needs a smaller round, lower heat and a part-bake. Thin bases want high heat and your full attention. Verify the centre at 165°F / 74°C and eat it straight away.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
