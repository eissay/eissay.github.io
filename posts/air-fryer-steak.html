Air Fryer Steak: Times, Temperatures and the 3-Minute Rest Rule

Air fryer steak times by thickness, doneness temperatures from rare to well done, flipping and preheating, and the 145°F / 63°C plus 3-minute rest rule.

An air fryer will not give you a steakhouse crust. What it will give you is a steak cooked evenly through in about the time it takes to set a table, with no pan to deglaze and no smoke alarm. The trade is honest: convenience and consistency, in exchange for the hard sear a cast iron pan produces.

The rule: 145°F / 63°C, then rest 3 minutes#

USDA guidance for whole cuts of beef is 145°F / 63°C with a 3-minute rest. The rest is part of the standard, because the temperature holds and the juices redistribute while the meat sits.

Cut typeSafe internal temperature
Whole cuts of beef, pork, veal (steaks, chops, roasts)145°F / 63°C + 3 min rest
Ground beef, including burger patties160°F / 71°C
Reheated steak leftovers165°F / 74°C

That distinction matters more than anything else on this page. A whole steak can safely be served medium-rare. Ground beef cannot. Once beef is ground, surface bacteria are mixed through the interior, so the middle of a burger has to reach 160°F / 71°C no matter how it is cooked or who is asking. There is no medium-rare burger that is safe by the same standard as a medium-rare steak.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer, not the clock. Air fryer models differ in wattage, basket size and how close the element sits to the food, so every time below is a range. Finish with an instant-read thermometer, inserted into the thickest part and away from bone and fat. Cook to 145°F / 63°C or above for anyone in a higher-risk group: young children, pregnant people, older adults, and anyone with a weakened immune system.

Why 1 inch and 1.5 inches are not the same steak#

A one-inch steak and a one-and-a-half-inch steak differ by fifty percent in thickness, and that is closer to a hundred percent more time. The outer half-inch on each side cooks fast because the moving air hits it directly, while the center warms by conduction from the outside in.

So a one-inch steak can hit medium-rare in ten minutes while one-and-a-half inches needs fifteen to twenty. Cook both on one timer and you get one good steak and one overcooked one. Measure the thickness before you set the temperature.

ThicknessTemperatureTimeDoneness if you follow itNotes
3/4 inch400°F / 205°C6–9 minRare to medium-rareFlip at halfway
1 inch400°F / 205°C9–13 minMedium-rareCheck at 8 min
1.25 inches390°F / 199°C11–15 minMedium-rareFlip once
1.5 inches380°F / 193°C14–19 minMedium-rareCheck at 12 min
2 inches375°F / 190°C18–24 minMedium-rareRest 5 min, not 3

Thicker steaks want lower heat and more time. High heat on a two-inch steak blackens the outside while the center stays cool.

Doneness temperatures#

Pull the steak a few degrees below your target and let the rest carry it the last bit.

DonenessTarget temperatureNotes
Rare120–125°F / 49–52°CBelow USDA minimum
Medium-rare130–135°F / 54–57°CBelow USDA minimum
Medium140–145°F / 60–63°CAt the USDA minimum
Medium-well150–155°F / 66–68°CAbove the minimum
Well done160°F / 71°C and aboveDry without careful timing

Here is the honest part. The USDA figure of 145°F / 63°C is a minimum for safety, not a recommendation for flavor. Everything under it is a deliberate trade that some people make and others do not. Serve 145°F / 63°C or above to anyone in a higher-risk group, as listed in the warning above.

Preheat and flip: the two things that decide the outcome#

Preheat. Three to five minutes at the cooking temperature, empty. A cold basket dries the surface before it browns, which is how you get gray steak.

Flip once. One flip at the halfway point is right for nearly every thickness. Flipping repeatedly cools the surface and slows browning.

Dry the surface. Pat the steak dry and oil the meat, not the basket.

Five mistakes that ruin air fryer steak#

  1. Cooking to time instead of temperature. Thickness beats every chart, including this one.
  2. Skipping the rest. Cutting at once loses the juice onto the plate.
  3. Overcrowding. Two steaks need two batches or a bigger basket.
  4. Going straight from the fridge. Twenty minutes on the counter gives a more even cook.
  5. Expecting a crust. Use a grill pan for thirty seconds per side afterward if you want one.

Frequently asked questions#

Can I cook a frozen steak in an air fryer?

It works, but unevenly. Add roughly 8–12 minutes to the times above and check the temperature early and often. Thawing first is better.

Do I need to rest the steak for exactly 3 minutes?

Three minutes is the USDA rest that goes with 145°F / 63°C. Five minutes is better for steaks an inch and a half or thicker.

What temperature for a medium-rare air fryer steak?

Set the appliance to 400°F / 205°C for a one-inch steak and pull it at 130–135°F / 54–57°C, understanding that this sits below the USDA minimum of 145°F / 63°C.

Can I cook steak and vegetables together?

Give the vegetables a 5–8 minute head start, and keep them in a single layer so the steak still gets direct air.

The short version#

Measure the thickness, preheat, oil the steak, flip once at halfway, and pull it at your target temperature with a thermometer. Rest it three minutes. That is the whole method.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
