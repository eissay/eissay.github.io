Air Fryer Turkey Breast: Times by Weight for Bone-In and Boneless

Air fryer turkey breast times by weight for bone-in, boneless, fresh and frozen, with brining, the 165°F / 74°C rule and why a whole turkey will not fit.

A turkey breast is the sensible answer to a holiday bird in an air fryer. It is small enough to fit, it cooks in about an hour, and it is almost all white meat, which is the part everyone reaches for first.

A whole turkey is not an option in a normal air fryer, and that is a size fact rather than a technique problem. Below are the weights that do fit, the times they need, and the fix for a whole bird.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Wattage and basket size change the clock, but 165°F / 74°C does not change.

The rule: 165°F / 74°C for all poultry#

PoultrySafe internal temperature
Turkey breast, bone-in or boneless165°F / 74°C
Ground turkey and turkey sausage165°F / 74°C
Reheated turkey leftovers165°F / 74°C

USDA sets one number for poultry, and it does not change for white meat, dark meat, ground turkey or a reheated plate. There is no medium-rare turkey and no pink center that is safe by a lower standard.

Probe the thickest part of the breast, away from the bone. Bone conducts heat differently and reads high, so a probe touching bone can tell you the turkey is done when the meat around it is not. If you are cooking a stuffed breast, the stuffing must also reach 165°F / 74°C.

Bone-in takes longer per pound than boneless because the bone blocks heat and adds mass that has to come up to temperature. A six-pound bone-in half breast is close to the limit of what most five- to six-quart baskets will hold, and it needs a lower temperature so the outside does not dry before the center is done.

For a boneless roast, tie it if it is loose so the thickness is even. An uneven roast finishes at the thin end long before the thick middle, and by the time the middle reads 165°F / 74°C the thin end is sliced meat for sandwiches.

Brining, and why it is worth the time#

Turkey breast is lean white meat with little fat to protect it, and the air fryer moves hot air across the surface constantly. A wet brine adds water and seasoning, and it is the single biggest improvement you can make to the result.

For a simple brine, dissolve one-half cup of kosher salt and one-quarter cup of sugar in one gallon of cold water. Submerge the breast and refrigerate for four to twelve hours. Rinse it, pat it completely dry, and let it sit uncovered in the refrigerator for an hour so the skin dries. Dry skin browns; wet skin steams.

Do not brine a turkey breast that is already enhanced, injected or self-basting. Read the label, because those products have salt and water added and brining them makes the meat salty rather than juicy.

A dry brine works too: rub the breast with salt and leave it uncovered in the refrigerator overnight. It seasons the meat and dries the surface with less counter space.

Why a whole turkey does not fit#

A whole turkey needs a cavity of hot air around it, not just a basket big enough to hold it. In a round or square air fryer basket the bird touches the sides and the element guard, and the air cannot circulate around it. The outside overcooks while the center stays cold, and the top skin can scorch against the heating element.

Two workable answers exist. The first is a turkey breast, which is what this page is about. The second is spatchcocking: remove the backbone with kitchen shears, press the bird flat, and cook it in two halves or in a large single layer.

A spatchcocked turkey that has been flattened to a fairly even thickness cooks in roughly 80–110 minutes at 330–350°F / 166–177°C in a large air fryer or a countertop oven with an air fry setting. Flattening is what makes it possible: even thickness means the breast and the thighs can reach 165°F / 74°C at close to the same time. Even then, check the bird against the element guard before you close the basket, and cook it in two batches if it does not clear.

A short note for Thanksgiving and Christmas#

Holiday cooking in an air fryer means one bird at a time and a plan for the oven. A four- pound boneless breast takes about 45–60 minutes from a preheated basket, which is faster than a full-size oven and frees the oven for sides. Cook it early, tent it with foil, and hold it at a safe hot temperature rather than letting it sit out on the counter. Reheat any leftover turkey to 165°F / 74°C.

If your gathering needs a whole bird for the table, spatchcock it in the oven and use the air fryer for a second breast. Two smaller pieces of meat cook more predictably than one large one.

Five mistakes with air fryer turkey breast#

  1. Probing the bone. Bone reads high, so you stop cooking too early.
  2. Cooking it cold from the refrigerator. Let it sit out 20 minutes so the center is not fighting the clock.
  3. Crowding the basket. The breast needs air on all sides, or the bottom steams.
  4. Slicing immediately. Rest 10–15 minutes so the juices redistribute instead of running onto the board.
  5. Reheating at high heat. Use a lower temperature until hot, and reheat leftovers to 165°F / 74°C.

Frequently asked questions#

How long does a turkey breast take in an air fryer?

A three-pound boneless roast takes 40–50 minutes at 350°F / 177°C. A four- to five-pound bone-in half breast takes 55–70 minutes at 340°F / 171°C. Always finish by probing the thickest part for 165°F / 74°C.

Bone-in or boneless for an air fryer?

Boneless is easier: it cooks faster, fits smaller baskets and slices cleanly. Bone-in has more flavor and stays juicier, but it needs a lower temperature, more time and a larger basket.

Can I cook a frozen turkey breast in an air fryer?

Only if the label says it is formulated to cook from frozen. Otherwise thaw it in the refrigerator, which takes about 24 hours for a three- to four-pound breast. A raw frozen breast cooks unevenly and is hard to verify with a thermometer.

Can I cook a whole turkey in an air fryer?

Not a standard 12- to 20-pound bird. There is no room for air to circulate and the top touches the element. Spatchcock the turkey instead, or cook a breast and reserve the oven for the rest.

Do I need to baste a turkey breast in the air fryer?

No. Opening the basket drops the temperature and the moving air already browns the surface. If the skin is browning too fast, tent it loosely with foil and lower the temperature.

The short version#

Choose a boneless roast of three to four pounds or a bone-in half breast of four to five, brine it if you have time, dry the skin, and cook it at 340–350°F / 171–177°C for 40–70 minutes depending on the cut. Rest it for 10–15 minutes, and probe the thickest part for 165°F / 74°C before it goes to the table.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
