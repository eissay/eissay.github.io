Air Fryer Brussels Sprouts: Halved, Charred and Crisp

Air fryer brussels sprouts times for fresh halved and frozen sprouts, with 380°F / 193°C settings, cut-side-down rule and the char that converts skeptics.

Brussels sprouts are the vegetable that converts the most skeptics when an air fryer is involved: halved sprouts cooked cut side down caramelize their flat faces into something nutty and sweet — nothing like the boiled gloom that gave them a bad reputation.

The variable that decides everything is the cut face. This chart covers fresh and frozen sprouts and the rules that build the char.

Times vary by appliance model. Sprout size and basket crowding change the clock — watch the first batch and adjust.

Air fryer brussels sprouts chart#

Halve every sprout through the core, toss with about one teaspoon of oil per cup, and lay them cut side down in one layer. The flat face against the hot basket is what caramelizes.

Doneness is texture: crisp-tender with a deep-gold, charred cut face. A skewer should slide through the core with slight resistance. Underdone sprouts squeak; overdone ones turn olive and bitter.

The cut-side-down rule#

The char lives on the flat face. Two habits build it:

  • Cut side down, and leave them alone. Sprouts flipped too often never caramelize — one shake at halfway is enough.
  • One layer with air between. A crowded basket steams the sprouts in their own vapor; two half-full batches beat one packed basket.

The oil rule from the site's vegetables chart applies here: about one teaspoon per cup. More fries the leaves greasy; less leaves them dry and pale.

Frozen versus fresh#

Frozen sprouts carry ice, so they want the hotter setting and a single flat layer — the ice steams off first, then the browning begins. Fresh halved sprouts caramelize more deeply because the cut face meets the basket dry.

StateSettingResult
Fresh, halved380°F / 193°CDeeper caramelization, nuttier flavor
Frozen, whole400°F / 205°CEven cook, milder char

Thawing frozen sprouts is never worth it — they cook from frozen at the same time and thawing turns them soft before they ever crisp.

Five mistakes with brussels sprouts#

  1. Cooking them whole. Halving doubles the caramelized surface; whole sprouts steam inside.
  2. Cut side up. The flat face must meet the basket; that is where the char lives.
  3. Overcrowding. A packed basket steams in its own vapor; two batches beat one.
  4. Overcooking past crisp-tender. Olive-green and bitter means too long — pull at golden.
  5. Balsamic or honey before cooking. Sugary glazes scorch; glaze in the last two minutes or after.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do brussels sprouts take in an air fryer?

12–14 minutes at 380°F / 193°C for fresh halved sprouts, cut side down, shaken once. Frozen sprouts need 10–15 minutes at 400°F / 205°C.

Do I need to cut brussels sprouts in half?

Yes — halving exposes the flat face that caramelizes and lets heat reach the core. Whole sprouts steam inside their leaves.

Why are my sprouts bitter and olive-green?

They overcooked or steamed in a crowded basket. Pull at crisp-tender with a golden cut face, and spread in one layer with air between.

Do I trim brussels sprouts first?

Trim the stem end and remove any loose or yellowed outer leaves — one pass with a knife per sprout.

What goes on sprouts after cooking?

Balsamic glaze, honey, shaved parmesan or toasted almonds — all after the heat, so nothing scorches.

Brussels sprouts reward the cut face: halved, cut side down, one layer, pulled at crisp-tender. The air fryer turns the most maligned vegetable into the one people fight over.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
