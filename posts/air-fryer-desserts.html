Air Fryer Desserts Chart (Cookies, Cakes and More)

Air fryer desserts chart with times and temperatures for cookies, mug cakes, brownies, cinnamon rolls, donuts, baked apples, s'mores and churros.

Dessert is the hardest thing to cook in an air fryer, and it is not close. A savoury dish tells you when it is done with colour and a thermometer. A cookie tells you nothing until you bite it.

The heating element sits inches from the food, the fan pushes dry air across the surface, and the basket is small. Cakes and cookies set on the outside while the middle is still batter. This is the category where a chart is a starting point and nothing more.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. For desserts the thermometer measures the air, not the food: there is no safe internal temperature for a cookie. Use a toothpick.

The rule that matters more than any number on this page#

There is no safe internal temperature for dessert. USDA internal-temperature guidance covers meat, poultry, eggs and leftovers, not cake batter, so anyone quoting you "the internal temperature of a brownie" is inventing a number.

That leaves you two checks that actually work:

  • Start checking early. If the chart says 15–18 min, look at 12 min. The last three minutes are where desserts go from underdone to ruined.
  • Use the toothpick test. Insert a wooden toothpick or skewer into the centre and pull it out. A few moist crumbs are done. Wet batter is not.

Cakes with eggs still need to be fully set, because raw egg is a food-safety issue in the same way it is in any other dish. If the centre is liquid, keep cooking in 1–2 min steps.

Temperatures are given as ranges on purpose. A 1500-watt basket and a 1800-watt oven with a small drawer behave differently, and dark non-stick pans bake faster than pale metal ones.

How to run each dessert without wrecking it#

Refrigerated cookie dough. Slice it cold, keep 1 inch between pieces and cook in a single layer. Dough that warms up on the counter spreads into one sheet. Cold dough holds its shape. If you use a parchment liner, cut it so air still moves and weight it with the dough.

Mug cakes. A straight-sided mug heats more evenly than a wide shallow one. Fill it no more than two-thirds, because the batter climbs. Ceramic holds heat longer than silicone, so it may need the longer end of the range.

Small cakes and brownies. Use the smallest pan that fits with space around it. A 6-inch round pan or two ramekins beat one deep tin, because the centre is closer to the moving air. Lower the temperature rather than the time when the top darkens too fast.

Cinnamon rolls. One layer, spiral side up, with a gap between rolls so they can expand. Frost them after they come out, never before, or the icing slides off and burns.

Donuts from canned biscuits. Flatten each biscuit into a ring, cut a hole with a bottle cap or your thumb, and cook in a single layer. They firm up fast, so check at 5 min. Toss in cinnamon sugar while hot so it sticks.

Baked apples. Halved apples cook more evenly than whole ones, which take far longer than most baskets can handle. A firm apple keeps its shape; a soft one collapses into sauce.

S'mores. Assemble on a small foil or parchment square for easy removal. Watch them the whole time. Marshmallows go from toasted to black in well under a minute at 350°F / 177°C.

Churros. Piping wet dough straight into a hot basket is messy and it spreads. Pipe onto parchment, freeze for 20 min until firm, then cook. Frozen churros also hold their ridges, which is the whole point of a churro.

Cooling and storage#

Most air-fried desserts firm up as they cool, because the structure keeps setting for several minutes after the heat stops. A cookie that looks underdone at 8 min may be perfect at 12 min. Take it out, wait 5 min on a rack, then judge it.

Cool anything you are storing on a rack, not in the basket. Trapped steam under the basket turns a crisp edge soft. Store covered at room temperature for a day or two, or refrigerate anything with a cream, custard or fruit filling and eat it within 2–3 days. Leftover baked goods from the refrigerator are eaten cold, not reheated, and any dish you do reheat should reach 165°F / 74°C.

Five mistakes with air fryer desserts#

  1. Using the savoury temperature. A 400°F / 205°C basket burns sugar and chocolate before the centre sets. Drop the heat and add time.
  2. Skipping the preheat. Cold air and cold dough give you a flat, pale cookie. Two to three minutes of preheat is enough.
  3. Crowding the basket. Two layers of dough fuse into a slab and the middle stays raw.
  4. Trusting the timer over the toothpick. Times vary by model more than any other category, and this one has no thermometer backup.
  5. Opening the drawer every minute. It drops the temperature each time. Check at the earliest time in the range, then leave it alone.

Frequently asked questions#

Can I bake cookies in an air fryer?

Yes, at 310–340°F / 154–171°C for 7–11 min depending on the dough. Keep them in one layer and check two to three minutes before the end of the range.

What temperature do I use for an air fryer cake?

Lower than you expect, around 300–320°F / 149–160°C, because the element is close to the top of the batter. The centre sets before the crust darkens at that heat.

Why is my air fryer cake raw in the middle?

The pan is too deep or too full, or the temperature is too high. Use a shallower pan, fill it two-thirds, lower the heat and give it more time.

How do I know when a dessert is done without a thermometer?

Use the toothpick test. Moist crumbs mean done, wet batter means keep going. There is no internal-temperature standard for baked desserts.

Can I cook frozen cookie dough straight from the freezer?

Yes, and it is easier than thawed dough because it spreads less. Add 1–3 min to the range and check at the earliest time.

The short version#

Air fryer desserts need lower heat, more patience and an early check. Use a toothpick, not a thermometer, and treat 300–340°F / 149–171°C as your baking zone. Cookies, brownies and cinnamon rolls work well in small batches. Anything with a wet batter in a deep pan does not.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
