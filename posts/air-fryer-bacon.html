Air Fryer Bacon: Times and Temperatures That Actually Work

Air fryer bacon times by thickness and doneness, with no-oil layouts, safe handling of hot grease, thick-cut and turkey bacon guidance.

Bacon is the food that convinces people to keep an air fryer on the counter: no splatter across the stovetop, and a strip that is crisp at the edges and still chewy in the middle if that is how you like it.

It also produces more grease than almost anything else you will cook, and that grease is the whole story. Handle it correctly and bacon is simple.

Two warnings before the chart#

First, times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer and your eyes, not the clock. Air fryer models differ in wattage, basket size and how close the element sits to the food, so a time that works in a 5.8-quart basket can burn bacon in a 2-quart one. Every time below is a range, and the last two minutes are yours to judge.

Second, bacon is cured and smoked before it reaches your kitchen, so it is judged by appearance rather than by a target temperature. Turkey bacon is the exception: it is ground poultry, and ground poultry is cooked to 165°F / 74°C like any other ground meat.

Bacon typeSafe internal temperature
Turkey bacon165°F / 74°C
Regular or thick-cut pork baconCook until crisp; judge by texture
Reheated bacon leftovers165°F / 74°C

No oil, and the layouts that stop sticking#

Bacon needs no oil at all. It releases its own fat within a minute or two, and that fat fries it. Adding oil gives you a greasier basket, not a better strip.

The sticking problem is mechanical, not a matter of fat. Three layouts work:

LayoutBest forWhy it works
Single layer, strips not touchingRegular and thick-cutAir reaches every surface
Over-easy weaveThick-cut, curly stripsStops strips curling into the element
Rack insert over the basketVery lean or turkey baconFat drips clear of the food

Never stack strips. Two layers means the middle stays limp and pale while the outside burns.

Bacon times by thickness and doneness#

Start checking two minutes before the low end of each range. Bacon keeps cooking for a few seconds after the basket comes out.

ThicknessTemperatureTimeResult
Regular cut400°F / 205°C8–10 minCrisp, classic
Regular cut380°F / 193°C9–12 minChewy center
Thick cut380°F / 193°C10–14 minCrisp edges, chewy middle
Thick cut375°F / 190°C13–16 minFully crisp
Turkey bacon380°F / 193°C7–10 minFollow package directions
Canadian bacon375°F / 190°C6–9 minAlready cooked, just heat

Thick-cut strips need a lower temperature than regular cut, which surprises people. The higher heat sets the outside before the middle renders, so you get a burnt edge and a raw-tasting center.

The grease question#

Every batch of bacon leaves a pool of hot fat in the basket. Four rules keep that safe:

  1. Cool before you move it. Let the basket sit for several minutes. Hot grease causes severe burns and it does not look dangerous.
  2. Never pour it down the sink. It solidifies in the drain. Pour it into a metal can once cool, or wipe it out with paper towel and bin it.
  3. Keep the basket level. Carrying a full basket at an angle sends grease toward your hand or the heating element.
  4. Do not line with a solid sheet. Parchment that lifts into the element is a fire risk. If you use a liner, use one that is perforated or sit the food on a rack above it.

A basket with a removable crisper plate drains better than a flat one. If yours is flat, tip the basket carefully at the end and let the fat collect at one corner.

Five mistakes that ruin air fryer bacon#

  1. Stacking strips. The single most common cause of uneven bacon.
  2. Starting cold without preheating. Bacon in a cold basket renders slowly and sticks.
  3. Adding oil. It has enough fat of its own.
  4. Walking away at minute eight. The window between crisp and burnt is about 60 seconds.
  5. Setting the temperature too high for thick cut. Lower heat, longer time, better strip.

Frequently asked questions#

Do I need to preheat the air fryer for bacon? Yes. About 3 minutes at the cooking temperature. A preheated basket stops the first strips from sticking and gives a more even cook.

How much bacon fits in one batch? Only what fits in a single layer. In most 5–6 quart baskets that is four to six regular strips. Cook two batches rather than crowding one.

Can I cook bacon and eggs together? Not in the same layer. The bacon needs the full time and the eggs need a fraction of it. Cook the bacon first, then pour off most of the fat and cook the eggs in the same basket.

Is turkey bacon cooked the same way? Same layout, lower time. It is leaner, so it sticks more easily and can dry out fast. Check it at 7 minutes and use a thermometer if you want the 165°F / 74°C confirmation.

Can I save the leftover fat? Yes, once it has cooled. Strain it into a jar and refrigerate. Never leave it sitting in the basket overnight, and never pour it warm into a plastic container.

The short version#

Single layer, no oil, 380–400°F / 193–205°C depending on thickness, and check two minutes early. Cool the basket before you touch the grease, and the rest is easy.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
