Chicken is the food people most want to cook in an air fryer, and the food they most often get wrong. The window between juicy and sawdust is narrow, and it moves depending on how thick the piece is and whether it still has a bone.

So this chart gives ranges you can start from. The number that decides whether dinner is safe is not on this page at all — it is the one your thermometer reads.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Wattage and basket size change the clock, but 165°F / 74°C does not change.

Why time alone is unreliable for chicken#

A 6-ounce breast and an 8-ounce breast look almost identical in the basket. One finishes in 14 minutes and the other needs 20. Bone-in pieces cook slower near the bone. A crowded basket slows everything down because the air cannot circulate.

An air fryer also browns the outside long before the inside is done. That is why a piece can look perfect, smell perfect and still be undercooked in the center. Color, juices and firmness are all guesses. A thermometer is a measurement.

Here is what the measurement has to say, according to USDA:

Poultry and related foodsSafe internal temperature
Chicken breast, thighs, wings, drumsticks, whole chicken165°F / 74°C
Ground chicken and ground turkey165°F / 74°C
Breaded chicken, fresh or frozen165°F / 74°C
Chicken leftovers, reheated165°F / 74°C

Note what is missing: there is no rest time that substitutes for temperature, and there is no cut of chicken where 160°F / 71°C is acceptable. Poultry is 165°F / 74°C across the board. Probe the thickest part, away from bone, fat and the pan's hot spot.

A whole chicken only works in a large basket, and only if there is space around it for air to move. If the bird touches the element guard, spatchcock it and cook it in two halves instead — 35–45 minutes at 380°F / 193°C, checked at the thickest part of the thigh.

Frozen versus thawed: what actually changes#

Thawed chicken gives you more control. You can season it through, pound it to an even thickness and predict the time. Frozen breaded chicken gives you speed and a crisp coating, at the cost of not knowing the exact internal temperature until you probe it.

If you are cooking raw chicken you froze yourself, thaw it in the refrigerator — never on the counter. A thin breast thaws overnight; a whole bird needs closer to 24 hours. Once thawed, treat it like fresh chicken and use the thawed chart.

For anything breaded, skip the oil. The coating is already formulated to crisp, and adding oil makes it greasy and soft.

Five mistakes with air fryer chicken#

  1. Going by the clock. A range tells you when to start checking, not when to stop.
  2. Probing the bone. Bone heats differently and reads high. Aim for the thickest flesh.
  3. Overcrowding. Two layers of chicken means uneven cooking and a cold spot in the middle.
  4. Skipping the rest. Five minutes on a board lets the juices settle. It does not replace reaching 165°F / 74°C.
  5. Cranking the heat to save time. Above 400°F / 205°C the outside burns before the center catches up.

Frequently asked questions#

What temperature do I cook chicken in an air fryer? Most cuts do well at 375–400°F / 190–205°C. Whatever you choose, the chicken must reach 165°F / 74°C in the thickest part before you serve it.

How long does chicken breast take in an air fryer? 18–22 minutes at 375°F / 190°C for a boneless breast, flipped at halfway. A thick breast may need the longer end; a thin one may finish sooner. Check at 16 minutes.

Can I cook frozen raw chicken in an air fryer? Only if the package says it is formulated to cook from frozen. Otherwise thaw it in the refrigerator first. Raw frozen pieces cook unevenly and are hard to verify.

Is 160°F / 71°C good enough for chicken? No. USDA sets 165°F / 74°C for all poultry, including ground chicken. If your thermometer reads 160°F / 71°C, keep cooking and probe again.

Do I need oil on chicken? For skin-on pieces, a light rub helps browning. Breaded frozen chicken needs none.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
