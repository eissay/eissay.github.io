Air Fryer Pigs in a Blanket: Crescent-Wrapped Cocktail Sausages

Air fryer pigs in a blanket at 375°F / 191°C, with the deep-golden dough rule, batch spacing and the make-ahead trick for parties.

Pigs in a blanket are the party food that disappears first, and the air fryer turns them into a batch-at-a-time snack machine: hot puffed dough, crisp seams, sausages heated through — with no oven preheating the whole kitchen for one tray.

Two ingredients and no thermometer homework. The franks are fully cooked before they are ever wrapped, which makes this one of the few hot appetizers where doneness is visible: the dough tells you everything.

Times vary by appliance model. There is no internal temperature to chase — the sausages are fully cooked before wrapping — so deep-golden, puffed dough is the only signal that matters.

The rule that matters#

375°F / 191°C for 10–12 min, and no preheat needed. The one physical rule is spacing: leave a gap between rolls so hot air reaches every side. Dough that touches its neighbor steams along the contact line and stays pale and soft exactly where the crowd is thickest.

Lay them seam side down so the spiral stays closed while the dough puffs. If a seam opens in the basket, the roll unspools and the exposed frank dries while the dough cooks unevenly.

Pinch every seam closed before the basket, and keep the dough to a single wrap around each frank. One spiral of crescent dough cooks through in the time above; a double-wrapped roll stays raw in the folds while the outside goes dark.

Reading the dough, not the clock#

Done pigs in a blanket are deep golden — darker than the pale blond that passes for done in an oven — puffed along the spiral, and firm when nudged. Pale dough at the end of the range means the middle is still soft; give the batch 2–4 more minutes and check again.

The color climbs fast at the end, so stay nearby for the last few minutes. Dough goes from golden to overdone quickly, and unlike a roast, there is no interior to protect — the surface is the whole dish.

Party timing and the make-ahead trick#

The make-ahead trick is what makes these practical for a crowd: wrap all the rolls in advance, set them on a tray, refrigerate, then cook straight from the fridge and add 1–2 min to the clock. Cold dough starts slow, which is the entire adjustment.

Cook in back-to-back batches and watch the second batch closely — a hot basket runs a touch faster than a cold one. Hold finished batches on a rack in a low oven, uncovered and in a single layer; covered or stacked rolls steam and lose the crisp within minutes.

Five mistakes with pigs in a blanket#

  1. Crowding the basket. Touching rolls steam along the contact line and stay pale.
  2. Loose seams. An unpinched spiral unrolls and cooks unevenly.
  3. Double-wrapping the dough. Extra layers stay raw in the folds while the outside darkens.
  4. Probing the sausage. It was fully cooked before wrapping; chasing a number only burns the dough.
  5. Piling them after cooking. Steam from the pile softens the crust; hold them on a rack.

Frequently asked questions#

What temperature do pigs in a blanket need?

375°F / 191°C for 10–12 min for fresh-wrapped rolls, with no preheat. Fridge-cold rolls take 11–14 min, and frozen store-bought versions take 12–15 min.

Do I need to preheat the air fryer?

No. They start fine in a cold basket, which saves minutes between party batches and makes the second batch the only one worth watching.

Is there an internal temperature for the sausages?

No. Cocktail franks are fully cooked before they are packaged and wrapped — you are heating dough and warming the filling inside it. The dough is the doneness guide: deep golden, puffed, firm at the seam.

Can I wrap them ahead of time?

Yes — wrap the rolls, hold the tray in the refrigerator, then cook them cold and add 1–2 min to the clock. It is the difference between hovering over a basket during the party and dropping a tray in when guests arrive.

How do I keep a big batch warm?

A rack in a low oven, uncovered, in a single layer. The rack keeps steam away from the crust; covering or stacking turns party-crisp into party-soft fast.

The short version#

375°F / 191°C, 10–12 min, no preheat, space between rolls, seam side down. The sausages are already cooked — deep-golden dough tells you everything, and a fridge-cold tray just adds 1–2 min.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
