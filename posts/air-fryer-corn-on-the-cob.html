Air Fryer Corn on the Cob: Times for Husked, In-Husk and Frozen

Air fryer corn on the cob times for husked, in-husk and frozen corn, with butter and oil handling and a note on reheating leftover corn safely.

Corn on the cob is the easiest side an air fryer makes. There is no pot of boiling water, no big kettle to drain, and the kernels come out with browned, slightly chewy spots that boiled corn never gets.

It is also the one food on this site with no internal temperature to hit. Corn is a vegetable, and vegetables have no USDA safe internal temperature.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer for meat and poultry. For corn, use the clock and your eyes, and remember that wattage and basket size change the time.

There is no safe internal temperature for corn

This matters because it is a common question. USDA publishes safe internal temperatures for meat, poultry, fish, eggs and reheated leftovers, not for vegetables. Corn has no target number, so anyone quoting one for the kernels is inventing it.

What corn does have is a doneness you can see. The kernels turn bright yellow, some of them brown at the tips, and a kernel pressed with a fork gives slightly. If the kernels look pale and flat, they need more time.

The exception is leftover corn. Once corn has been cooked and refrigerated, reheating it puts it under the standard for reheated food, which is 165°F / 74°C.

Husked versus in-husk

Husked corn cooks faster and browns more. In-husk corn steams inside its own wrapper and comes out juicier, with a milder flavor and no browned spots.

For in-husk corn, pull the husk down without removing it, strip the silk, then pull the husk back up over the kernels. The husk becomes a handle and a shield at the same time. Soak the cob in cold water for ten minutes first so the husk does not scorch.

Frozen corn on the cob goes in straight from the freezer. There is no need to thaw it, and thawing it first only adds water to the basket.

Butter, oil and seasoning

Corn has almost no fat of its own, so something has to carry the seasoning and help the kernels brown.

For husked corn, brush or rub each cob with a thin coat of oil or melted butter before it goes in, and season with salt and pepper. A light coat is enough. Too much butter pools in the basket, smokes, and leaves the kernels greasy rather than browned.

For in-husk corn, put the fat inside. Peel the husk back, brush the kernels, add salt, and close the husk again. The husk traps the butter against the corn.

Butter burns at a lower temperature than oil, and an air fryer moves hot air directly over the surface. If you want butter flavor without scorching, cook the corn with oil and brush it with melted butter after it comes out of the basket. That gives you browning from the oil and butter flavor that never hit the element.

Cold butter straight from the refrigerator does not spread. Melt it, or soften it first.

Times vary by appliance model — use a thermometer. Corn has no internal temperature, but any meat you cook alongside it does.

Common mistakes with air fryer corn

  1. Peeling every husk by reflex. In-husk corn is juicier and needs no foil.
  2. Soaking for hours. Ten minutes is enough to keep the husk from burning.
  3. Drowning the cob in butter. It smokes and pools; a thin coat browns better.
  4. Stacking cobs. Two layers means the bottom cobs steam and stay pale.
  5. Leaving the silk on. Silk that stays on the cob does not soften, it just sticks to the kernels.

Reheating leftover corn

Cooked corn on the cob keeps in the refrigerator for three to five days. Reheat it in the air fryer at 350°F / 177°C for 4–6 minutes, turning once.

For loose kernels, spread them in a single layer at 350°F / 177°C for 4–5 minutes. They will not brown much, because they are already cooked, and that is fine.

Reheated leftovers are held to the 165°F / 74°C standard, so reheat corn until it is steaming hot rather than merely warm. If you are reheating corn along with leftover meat, the meat needs its own check: 165°F / 74°C for poultry and any reheated leftovers.

Frequently asked questions

How long does corn on the cob take in an air fryer?

10–14 minutes at 400°F / 205°C for husked corn, and 15–20 minutes at 400°F / 205°C when the husk is left on. Frozen cobs need 14–18 minutes without thawing.

Do I need to soak corn before air frying it?

Only if you leave the husk on. Ten minutes in cold water keeps the husk from scorching before the kernels are done. Husked corn needs no soaking.

Should I wrap corn in foil in the air fryer?

No. Foil blocks the moving air that browns the kernels, and loose foil can lift into the heating element. Cook the corn directly in the basket.

Can I cook corn and other food at the same time?

Only if the times match. Corn needs 10–14 minutes of its own, so give it the basket first or add it late. Cook meat separately and check it with a thermometer.

Is canned corn worth air frying?

Yes, for a quick side. Drain it well, pat it dry, spread it in a single layer, and give it 8–11 minutes at 390°F / 199°C. Wet corn steams instead of browning.

The short version

Husked corn: 400°F / 205°C for 10–14 minutes, turned once, with a thin coat of oil. In-husk corn: soaked ten minutes, then 15–20 minutes at 400°F / 205°C. Frozen cobs: straight in for 14–18 minutes. No internal temperature is needed for the corn itself, but reheat any leftovers to 165°F / 74°C.
