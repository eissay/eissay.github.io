Air Fryer Egg Cups: Times, Liners and Fillings

Air fryer egg cups at 350°F / 177°C for 10–14 minutes, with silicone versus foil liners, the 3/4 fill rule, and the 160°F / 71°C set-egg standard.

Egg cups — the muffin-tin omelets people bake in the oven — turn out beautifully in an air fryer. The small chamber heats the cups evenly from all sides, and a batch of six takes about the same time as one pan in a full oven. For a week of grab-and-go breakfasts, it is the fastest route there is.

The template is simple: whisked eggs, a handful of fillings, liners, and a short cook. The details that matter are how full you fill, which liners you use, and how you know the centers are set.

Times vary by appliance model and cup size. Eggs are done when fully set — cook egg dishes to 160°F / 71°C internal, the FDA and USDA standard for egg dishes.

The rule that matters#

An egg cup is done when the center is fully set, with no loose or runny liquid. A tall, domed top means nothing by itself — cups puff dramatically and can still be liquid inside. Cut the thickest cup in half or probe its center.

Fill every cup to 3/4 at most. Eggs foam as they heat, and a full cup puffs over the rim and onto the basket floor.

Silicone or foil liners#

Silicone cups are reusable, release cleanly, and bake the most evenly — they are the default choice. Foil liners hold their shape better for transport and are easier to fill on a counter, but they conduct heat into the base, so the bottoms brown sooner; start checking at the early end of the range with foil.

Paper muffin liners are the one to skip. They are flimsy in the fan's airflow and can scorch against the hot basket. A light spray of oil on silicone cups keeps even lean, cheese-heavy cups from sticking.

The puff-and-settle#

Whisked eggs trap air, and that air expands in the heat. Each cup rises like a soufflé, sometimes doubling in height, then settles back as the proteins set. Two things follow from that. First, fill to 3/4 or the puff has nowhere to go but over the rim. Second, never read height as doneness — a cup at its puffiest can still be unset in the middle, so judge by a cut center or 160°F / 71°C on the probe.

Five mistakes with air fryer egg cups#

  1. Filling the cups to the brim. Eggs puff hard; past 3/4 they overflow and burn onto the basket.
  2. Adding raw watery fillings. Mushrooms and thawed vegetables release water that keeps the center from setting.
  3. Underbaking. A puffed top is not a set center; confirm 160°F / 71°C or a clean cut through the thickest cup.
  4. Overbaking. Left too long, cups turn rubbery and their bottoms brown hard; pull at the early end once you know your basket.
  5. Popping them out hot. Cups tear when fresh from the heat; rest 2–3 min and they release cleanly.

Frequently asked questions#

How long do egg cups take in the air fryer?

10–14 minutes at 350°F / 177°C. Plain eggs finish at 10–12 min; cups loaded with vegetables or pre-cooked meat take 12–14 min.

What temperature for air fryer egg muffin cups?

350°F / 177°C for every filling and liner type. Higher heat browns the tops before the centers set.

Can you use paper liners in an air fryer?

They are not recommended — paper is flimsy in the airflow and can scorch. Silicone cups or foil liners hold up and release cleanly.

Why did my egg cups deflate?

Settling is normal as the trapped air cools. Cups that collapse a lot were underbaked or overfilled; fill to 3/4 and cook until the center is fully set.

How do you store and reheat egg cups?

Refrigerate them in a sealed container. Reheat 3–5 min at 350°F / 177°C, until steaming hot all the way through.

The short version#

350°F / 177°C, 10–14 min, fill every cup to 3/4, pre-cook the wet add-ins, and call them done at a fully set center — 160°F / 71°C on the probe.

Always verify doneness with an instant-read thermometer. Cooking times are starting points and vary by appliance model, wattage and basket size.
